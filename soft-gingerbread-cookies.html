Soft Gingerbread Cookies

Prep Time: 30 mins Cook Time: 10 mins Servings: about 24 cookies Category: Dessert

Illustrative image.

Gingerbread cookies are often either crisp to the point of breaking or soft enough to bend, and this recipe is firmly in the second camp. Molasses and brown sugar keep the crumb tender, while ginger, cinnamon and cloves give the dough its warm, dark flavour.

The dough needs to chill thoroughly before rolling, so plan for an hour in the fridge. It makes about twenty-four cookies, depending on the size of your cutters, and they keep well enough to decorate over a couple of days.

Ingredients

  • 3 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp baking soda
  • 1 tbsp ground ginger
  • 1 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 tsp ground cloves
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 3/4 cup (1 1/2 sticks) unsalted butter, softened
  • 3/4 cup packed brown sugar
  • 1 large egg
  • 1/2 cup unsulphured molasses
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract

Instructions

  1. Whisk the flour, baking soda, ginger, cinnamon, cloves and salt in a bowl.
  2. In a large bowl, beat the butter and brown sugar until light and fluffy, about 2 minutes. Beat in the egg, molasses and vanilla.
  3. Add the dry ingredients and mix on low until a soft dough forms. Divide in half, flatten into disks, wrap and refrigerate for at least 2 hours.
  4. Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C) and line baking sheets with parchment.
  5. Roll one disk on a lightly floured surface to about 1/4 inch thick. Cut out shapes and place them 1 inch apart on the sheets.
  6. Bake for 8 to 10 minutes, until the edges look set. Cool on the sheet for 5 minutes, then move to a wire rack. Decorate once completely cool.
  7. Chill the dough thoroughly. Warm dough spreads and loses its shape.
  8. Roll the dough evenly so the cookies bake at the same rate.
  9. For softer cookies, bake closer to 8 minutes. Bake longer for a crisper cookie.
  10. If the dough gets sticky while rolling, return it to the refrigerator for 10 minutes.
  11. Decorate with royal icing, or simple icing made from powdered sugar and milk.
  12. Roll the dough into balls, dip in sugar and bake for chewy gingerbread cookies instead of cut-outs.
  13. Add 1 tsp orange zest for a citrusy note.

Why this recipe works

Brown sugar and molasses both hold moisture, which is what keeps the cookies soft after baking. Chilling firmsthe butter so the shapes hold their edges, and rolling to an even thickness means every cookie bakes at the same rate.

Tips for success

  • Chill the dough for at least an hour, and return it to the fridge if it starts to feel sticky while you roll it.
  • Bake for about eight minutes for a soft cookie and closer to ten for a crisper one.
  • Let the cookies cool completely before icing, or the icing will run off the edges.

Substitutions that work

  • Blackstrap molasses is too bitter for this recipe; use unsulphured mild molasses.
  • Add a teaspoon of orange zest for a citrus note that works well with the spices.
  • For chewier cookies, roll the dough into balls, dip them in granulated sugar and bake without cutting shapes.

Storage and make-ahead

Store in an airtight container for a week, with parchment between layers if iced. The cookies soften further if you add a slice of bread to the container. Dough freezes for three months, and baked cookies freeze for two.

Serving suggestions

Serve with milk, coffee or mulled cider, and leave a few out for a holiday plate. They also make a good gift stacked in a tin with parchment between the layers.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my gingerbread cookies spread?

The dough was too warm. Chill it thoroughly, work quickly and bake one sheet at a time on a cool pan.

How do I make gingerbread cookies softer?

Bake them for less time and store them in an airtight container with a slice of bread. Pulling them from the oven while the centres still look slightly underdone also helps.

Can I make the dough ahead of time?

Yes. The dough keeps in the refrigerator for three days, or in the freezer for three months. Thaw overnight in the fridge before rolling.
