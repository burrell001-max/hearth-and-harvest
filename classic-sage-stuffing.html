Classic Sage Stuffing

Prep Time: 25 mins Cook Time: 50 mins Total: 75 mins Servings: 10

Good stuffing is crisp and golden on top, soft and flavorful underneath, and full of the savory smell of sage, thyme and butter. This version is baked in a casserole dish rather than inside the bird, which makes it easier to cook evenly and easier to prepare ahead.

Dry, cubed bread is the key. It soaks up the broth without turning mushy.

Ingredients

  • 1 loaf (about 14 to 16 oz) sturdy white or country bread, cut into 3/4-inch cubes (about 10 cups)
  • 1/2 cup (1 stick) unsalted butter, plus more for the dish
  • 2 cups chopped yellow onion
  • 1 cup chopped celery
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp chopped fresh sage (or 1 tsp dried)
  • 1 tbsp fresh thyme leaves (or 1 tsp dried)
  • 2 tbsp chopped fresh parsley
  • 1 1/2 to 2 cups low-sodium chicken or vegetable broth
  • 2 large eggs, beaten
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste

Instructions

  1. Dry the bread: spread the cubes on baking sheets and bake at 275°F for 30 to 40 minutes, or leave them out overnight, until dry but not browned.
  2. Raise the oven to 375°F (190°C) and butter a 9x13-inch baking dish.
  3. Melt the butter in a large skillet over medium heat. Cook the onion and celery for 8 to 10 minutes until soft. Add the garlic, sage and thyme and cook for 1 minute.
  4. In a large bowl, combine the bread, the vegetable mixture and the parsley. Season with salt and pepper.
  5. Whisk 1 1/2 cups of the broth with the eggs and pour over the bread. Toss gently, adding more broth until the bread is moist but not swimming.
  6. Spread in the dish, cover with foil and bake for 25 minutes. Remove the foil and bake 20 to 25 minutes more, until golden on top and 165°F (74°C) in the center.

Ingredient Notes and Substitutions

Bread

Any sturdy bread works. Avoid soft sandwich bread, which turns gummy. Cornbread makes a Southern-style version.

Broth

Use low-sodium so you can control the seasoning. Warm broth absorbs more evenly.

Herbs

Fresh herbs give the best flavor, but dried work. Use about one third the amount.

Tips for Success

  • Dry the bread properly. It is the difference between fluffy and soggy.
  • Add the broth gradually and stop when the bread is evenly moist.
  • Cover with foil first so it cooks through, then uncover to crisp the top.
  • Taste the vegetable mixture before combining and adjust the salt.

Variations

  • Add 1 lb cooked crumbled breakfast sausage.
  • Stir in 1 cup chopped sautéed mushrooms or tart apple.
  • Add 1/2 cup dried cranberries and chopped pecans.

Storage and Make-Ahead

Assemble up to a day ahead, cover and refrigerate, then add a few minutes to the baking time. Refrigerate leftovers for up to 4 days and reheat covered at 350°F with a splash of broth. Baked stuffing freezes well for up to 2 months.

Frequently Asked Questions

Stuffing or dressing?

They are often used interchangeably. Strictly, stuffing is cooked inside the bird, and dressing is baked separately.

Can I make it vegetarian?

Yes. Use vegetable broth.

Why is mine dry?

Add a bit more broth next time, and keep it covered with foil for the first half of baking.

Can I stuff the turkey with it?

I recommend baking it separately. Stuffing inside a bird must reach 165°F, which is hard to achieve without overcooking the meat.
