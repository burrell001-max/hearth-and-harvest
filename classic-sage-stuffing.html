Classic Sage Stuffing

Prep Time: 25 mins Cook Time: 50 mins Servings: 10 Category: Dinner

Stuffing is the side dish people argue about, and the arguments are usually about moisture. Too dry and it crumbles; too wet and it turns into bread pudding. The target is a savoury, herb-flecked bake that holds together on a fork but still has crisp edges.

This version bakes in a dish rather than inside a bird, which makes the timing far easier and lets the top crisp properly. It uses a full loaf of sturdy bread, plenty of butter, onion, celery, sage and thyme, and enough broth to moisten without soaking.

Ingredients

  • 1 loaf (about 14 to 16 oz) sturdy white or country bread, cut into 3/4-inch cubes (about 10 cups)
  • 1/2 cup (1 stick) unsalted butter, plus more for the dish
  • 2 cups chopped yellow onion
  • 1 cup chopped celery
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp chopped fresh sage (or 1 tsp dried)
  • 1 tbsp fresh thyme leaves (or 1 tsp dried)
  • 2 tbsp chopped fresh parsley
  • 1 1/2 to 2 cups low-sodium chicken or vegetable broth
  • 2 large eggs, beaten
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste

Instructions

  1. Dry the bread: spread the cubes on baking sheets and bake at 275°F for 30 to 40 minutes, or leave them out overnight, until dry but not browned.
  2. Raise the oven to 375°F (190°C) and butter a 9x13-inch baking dish.
  3. Melt the butter in a large skillet over medium heat. Cook the onion and celery for 8 to 10 minutes until soft. Add the garlic, sage and thyme and cook for 1 minute.
  4. In a large bowl, combine the bread, the vegetable mixture and the parsley. Season with salt and pepper.
  5. Whisk 1 1/2 cups of the broth with the eggs and pour over the bread. Toss gently, adding more broth until the bread is moist but not swimming.
  6. Spread in the dish, cover with foil and bake for 25 minutes. Remove the foil and bake 20 to 25 minutes more, until golden on top and 165°F (74°C) in the center.
  7. Dry the bread properly. It is the difference between fluffy and soggy.
  8. Add the broth gradually and stop when the bread is evenly moist.
  9. Cover with foil first so it cooks through, then uncover to crisp the top.
  10. Taste the vegetable mixture before combining and adjust the salt.
  11. Add 1 lb cooked crumbled breakfast sausage.
  12. Stir in 1 cup chopped sautéed mushrooms or tart apple.
  13. Add 1/2 cup dried cranberries and chopped pecans.

Why this recipe works

Drying the bread first is the most important step. Cubes that are dry on the surface absorb broth without collapsing, so the stuffing stays fluffy. Adding the broth gradually and stopping when the bread is evenly damp, not wet, keeps the texture right.

Tips for success

  • Dry the bread cubes in a low oven for half an hour, or leave them out overnight.
  • Cook the onion and celery until soft and slightly golden before mixing them in; raw vegetables release water in the oven.
  • Cover with foil for the first half of baking, then uncover so the top crisps.

Substitutions that work

  • Use vegetable broth instead of chicken broth to make the dish vegetarian.
  • Dried herbs work: use a teaspoon each of dried sage and thyme in place of the fresh amounts.
  • Add cooked crumbled sausage, sautéed mushrooms or chopped apple for a different version.

Storage and make-ahead

Refrigerate leftovers covered for three days. Reheat covered at 350 F for twenty minutes with a splash of broth to stop it drying out. Assemble the unbaked stuffing a day ahead and refrigerate; add ten minutes to the baking time.

Serving suggestions

Serve it with roast turkey, roast chicken or a Sunday pork roast, and spoon over a little gravy. It also works as a vegetarian main if you add mushrooms and a handful of chopped nuts.

Frequently asked questions

Can I put this stuffing inside the turkey?

It is safer to bake it separately. Stuffing inside a bird has to reach a safe temperature without the meat overcooking, and a separate dish gives a crisper top and more predictable timing.

Why is my stuffing mushy?

Too much broth, or bread that was too fresh. Dry the cubes properly and add the broth a little at a time, stopping when the bread is moist but not saturated.

Can I make stuffing ahead of time?

Yes. Assemble it up to a day ahead, cover and refrigerate, then bake just before serving. You can also freeze it baked for up to a month.
