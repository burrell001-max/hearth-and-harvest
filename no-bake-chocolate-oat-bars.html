No-Bake Chocolate Peanut Butter Oat Bars

Prep Time: 20 mins Cook Time: 0 mins Servings: 16 Category: Dessert

These bars need no oven, no thermometer and no special equipment beyond a saucepan and a square pan. Oats are bound with warm peanut butter, honey and coconut oil, pressed flat, and topped with a layer of chocolate that sets in the fridge.

They are also more forgiving than they look. The only real failure is a batch that crumbles when cut, and that is easy to avoid once you know what the mixture should feel like before it goes into the pan.

Ingredients

  • 2 1/2 cups old-fashioned rolled oats
  • 1 cup natural creamy peanut butter
  • 1/2 cup honey
  • 1/3 cup coconut oil
  • 2 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • 1 1/2 cups dark chocolate chips
  • 1 tbsp coconut oil, for the topping
  • Flaky sea salt, to finish

Instructions

  1. Line an 8-inch square pan with parchment, leaving an overhang on two sides so the bars can be lifted out later.
  2. Warm the peanut butter, honey and coconut oil in a saucepan over low heat, stirring until smooth and pourable. Take it off the heat and stir in the vanilla and salt.
  3. Tip the oats into the pan and fold until every oat is coated. The mixture should hold together when squeezed; if it crumbles, add another tablespoon of peanut butter.
  4. Press the mixture firmly into the lined pan. Use the base of a measuring cup to compact it hard, which is what stops the bars falling apart when cut.
  5. Melt the chocolate chips with the tablespoon of coconut oil and spread over the base. Refrigerate for at least 2 hours, until the chocolate is set and the base is firm.
  6. Lift the slab out by the parchment, sprinkle with flaky salt and cut into 16 squares with a hot knife. Store in the fridge.

Why this recipe works

Coconut oil is what makes these set firm in the fridge, since it is solid at room temperature and firms up the whole base. Honey does the same job while adding sweetness and keeping the bars chewy rather than brittle. Pressing the mixture down hard with a flat object compacts the oats so the bars hold together when cut, and chilling before cutting means the chocolate does not crack.

Tips for success

  • Warm the peanut butter mixture only until it pours. Boiling it makes the bars greasy and crumbly.
  • Use old-fashioned rolled oats, not instant. Instant oats turn to paste and the bars lose all texture.
  • Do the squeeze test: press a spoonful in your hand, and if it holds its shape the mixture is right.
  • Press with the base of a measuring cup, working from the centre outwards, until the surface is genuinely flat.
  • Chill for a full two hours before cutting, and use a hot knife wiped clean between cuts.

Substitutions that work

  • Butter can replace the coconut oil, though the bars need to stay refrigerated to hold their shape.
  • Maple syrup can stand in for honey, with a slightly looser set and a milder flavour.
  • Almond butter or sunflower seed butter makes a good alternative to peanut butter; the latter keeps it nut-free.
  • Add a handful of raisins, chopped nuts or a pinch of flaky salt to the base for variation.

Storage and make-ahead

Store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to two weeks, or freeze for three months, layered with parchment. They soften quickly at room temperature, so bring them out just before serving. Wrapped individually, they travel well in a lunch bag with a cold pack.

Serving suggestions

Cut into 16 squares for a snack or 9 larger bars for dessert. They go well with coffee, in lunchboxes, or crumbled over yogurt, and a small square alongside a scoop of vanilla ice cream makes a very quick dessert.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to use coconut oil?

No. Melted butter works, but the bars will be softer and need to stay refrigerated. Coconut oil gives the firmest set at room temperature.

Why are my bars crumbly?

The mixture was too dry, usually because the peanut butter was stiff or the oats were packed rather than measured loosely. Add another tablespoon of peanut butter and press harder.

How long do they keep?

Two weeks in the fridge in a sealed container, or three months in the freezer. They should not be kept at room temperature for more than a few hours.
