Greek Salad

Prep Time: 15 mins Cook Time: 0 mins Servings: 4 Category: Healthy

A Greek salad is not a lettuce salad with feta on top. It is tomato, cucumber, onion, pepper and olives, dressed with good olive oil and a little vinegar, with a whole slab of feta laid over the top and a scatter of dried oregano.

The quality of the tomatoes matters more than anything else here, so make it in summer when they taste of something. Four servings take fifteen minutes and no cooking at all.

Ingredients

  • 4 large tomatoes, cut into wedges
  • 1 English cucumber, sliced into half-moons
  • 1/2 red onion, thinly sliced
  • 1 green bell pepper, sliced
  • 1/2 cup Kalamata olives
  • 7 oz block feta cheese
  • 1/4 cup extra virgin olive oil
  • 2 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Instructions

  1. Combine the tomatoes, cucumber, red onion, green pepper, and olives in a large bowl.
  2. Whisk together the olive oil, red wine vinegar, oregano, salt, and pepper in a small bowl.
  3. Pour the dressing over the vegetables and toss gently to combine.
  4. Transfer to a serving platter and place the block of feta on top.
  5. Drizzle a little extra olive oil and a sprinkle of oregano over the feta and serve immediately.

Why this recipe works

Salting the tomatoes and letting them sit for ten minutes draws out juice that becomes part of the dressing. Keeping the feta in one piece rather than crumbling it means each serving gets a clean, salty bite instead of a milky coating over everything.

Tips for success

  • Use ripe room-temperature tomatoes, cut into wedges rather than diced, so they hold their shape.
  • Slice the red onion thinly and rinse it under cold water to take the edge off its bite.
  • Dress the salad just before serving, and add the feta last so it does not break up.

Substitutions that work

  • Any good block feta works; barrel-aged feta is saltier and more crumbly than supermarket versions.
  • Dried oregano is traditional, but fresh oregano or a little thyme is a nice alternative in summer.
  • Add capers or a few sliced peperoncini if you want more acidity and heat.

Storage and make-ahead

The salad is best fresh. Undressed components can be prepared a few hours ahead and refrigerated separately. Once dressed, it keeps for a day but the cucumber softens and the tomatoes release more liquid.

Serving suggestions

Serve alongside grilled lamb, chicken souvlaki or lemon potatoes. With bread and a bowl of olives, it makes a light lunch on its own.

The tomatoes decide everything

This salad is only as good as the tomatoes in it. Look for fruit that smells sweet at the stem and feels heavy for its size, and keep it at room temperature rather than in the refrigerator, which dulls the flavour. Cut the tomatoes into thick wedges, salt them in the bowl and leave them for ten minutes while you prepare everything else; the juice that collects becomes part of the dressing. Cherry tomatoes can be halved instead, though they are firmer and less juicy. Out of season, a mix of cherry tomatoes and a good quality tinned plum tomato, drained well, is better than pale winter salad tomatoes.

Frequently asked questions

Does a real Greek salad have lettuce?

No. A traditional horiatiki is tomato, cucumber, onion, pepper, olives and feta. Lettuce is an addition that appears in some restaurants but is not part of the classic dish.

How do I stop the salad getting watery?

Salt the tomatoes shortly before serving, drain the cucumber if it is very wet, and dress the salad at the last moment.

Can I make Greek salad ahead of time?

Prepare the vegetables and dressing separately up to a day ahead, then combine just before serving so everything stays crisp.
