Easy Peppermint Bark

Prep Time: 20 mins Cook Time: 5 mins Servings: about 24 pieces Category: Dessert

Illustrative image.

Peppermint bark is a holiday gift in edible form. A layer of semisweet chocolate sets, a layer of white chocolate goes over it, and crushed candy canes scatter across the top. It needs no oven, only a microwave or a bowl over simmering water.

It makes about twenty-four pieces and takes twenty minutes of active work plus an hour of chilling. Wrap it in cellophane and it becomes a present.

Ingredients

  • 10 oz semisweet chocolate, chopped (or chips)
  • 10 oz white chocolate, chopped (or chips)
  • 1/2 tsp peppermint extract
  • 3/4 cup crushed candy canes (about 6 to 8 canes)

Instructions

  1. Line a 9x13-inch baking pan with parchment paper.
  2. Melt the semisweet chocolate in a microwave-safe bowl in 30-second bursts, stirring each time, until smooth. Spread evenly in the pan and refrigerate for 15 to 20 minutes, until firm.
  3. Melt the white chocolate the same way and stir in the peppermint extract.
  4. Spread the white chocolate over the dark layer and sprinkle immediately with the crushed candy canes, pressing lightly.
  5. Chill for about 1 hour, until completely set. Break into pieces.
  6. Melt the chocolate gently and avoid getting any water in it, which makes it seize.
  7. Let the dark layer firm up before spreading the white chocolate, so the layers stay separate.
  8. Crush candy canes in a zip bag with a rolling pin.
  9. Reverse the layers or use milk chocolate.
  10. Add a few drops of food coloring to the white chocolate or sprinkle with sea salt.

Why this recipe works

Letting the dark layer set fully before adding the white chocolate keeps the two bands distinct rather than marbled. Peppermint extract is stirred into the dark chocolate as well as the white, and crushing the candy canes finely means the pieces stick to the chocolate rather than falling off.

Tips for success

  • Chop the chocolate finely so it melts evenly and quickly.
  • Keep every utensil completely dry. A drop of water makes melted chocolate seize into a grainy paste.
  • Press the crushed candy into the white chocolate while it is still tacky, not fully set.

Substitutions that work

  • Milk chocolate can replace the semisweet for a sweeter bark.
  • Peppermint extract can be swapped for a few drops of vanilla if you prefer a milder flavour.
  • Sprinkle flaky sea salt over the top for a sweet-salty version.

Storage and make-ahead

Store in an airtight container in a cool place for two weeks, with parchment between layers. Refrigerate in warm weather, since the chocolate softens. It freezes for two months and thaws quickly.

Serving suggestions

Serve on a holiday platter with cookies, or pack it in small bags as a gift. It is also good broken over vanilla ice cream.

Melting chocolate without trouble

Chocolate is forgiving if you keep it dry and keep the heat low. Melt it in short bursts, stirring after each one, and stop while a few lumps remain; the residual heat will melt them as you stir. A glass bowl can hold heat and scorch the chocolate at the bottom, so use a ceramic or metal bowl if you have one. Work with a pan that fits your tray, and line it with parchment that overhangs on two sides so you can lift the slab out later. Score the bark lightly with a knife before it sets if you want neater pieces.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my chocolate seize?

Water got into the melted chocolate, or it was overheated. Start over with dry equipment and melt gently in short bursts, stirring between each.

Do I have to use peppermint extract?

It is what gives the bark its flavour, but you can leave it out or reduce it to a quarter teaspoon for a milder result.

Can I make peppermint bark without a microwave?

Yes. Melt the chocolate in a bowl set over a pan of barely simmering water, making sure the bowl does not touch the water.
