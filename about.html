About Hearth & Harvest

A small recipe site for people who cook at home, written and run by Kent B Williams, and funded by advertising.

Why this site exists

Many recipe pages open with several hundred words of story before you reach the ingredients. Hearth & Harvest is arranged the other way round. The recipe comes first, the steps are numbered, and the notes afterwards explain the details that actually change the result: why the cake batter is deliberately thin, why the garlic has to stay pale gold, why the potatoes start in cold water.

The aim is a collection you can cook from on a Tuesday night without scrolling through a memoir, and without wondering whether the timing on the page was written for a professional range.

What the site does

The site publishes a working collection of home recipes across five everyday categories: dinner, breakfast, dessert, healthy and quick meals. Every recipe page lists ingredients, preparation and cooking times, servings, numbered instructions, substitutions that work, storage advice and answers to the questions that tend to come up while cooking.

The homepage has a search box and category filters that narrow the recipe grid as you type. Both run entirely in the browser, so nothing you type is sent anywhere.

Who runs it

Hearth & Harvest is written and maintained by Kent B Williams. It is a one-person site: the recipes, the writing and the pages themselves come from the same place, and there is no editorial team, no test kitchen and no sponsored content. Recipes are chosen because they are the version worth keeping, not because a brand asked for them.

How the recipes are produced

Recipes are written for home equipment and supermarket ingredients. Quantities are given in cups and spoons, with temperatures in both Fahrenheit and Celsius, and the instructions describe what to look for as well as how long to wait, since ovens and pans vary more than timings admit.

Each page is a single static document: the ingredients, the steps and the notes live together, so there is no separate database that can drift out of step with what you read. When a recipe is corrected, the page itself is corrected. Ingredient amounts, times and serving counts are stated on the page rather than implied, and where a dish is easy to get wrong, the reason is explained instead of being left as a warning.

Photography

Images on this site exist to show the dish a method is aiming for. Some are licensed stock photographs; the newer ones are digital illustrations made for the site with an image generation tool. Either way they are illustrative rather than a record of one particular batch, so the crisp on your table will not look identical to the one on the page, because apples, pans and ovens differ.

Every image carries alt text describing what is in it, so the pages still work when read aloud or with images turned off. The provenance of each image is recorded in the media credits file kept with the site's source.

What stays on your device

There are no accounts, no sign-in and no contact forms that submit to a server owned by this site. Nothing you type into the search box leaves your browser, and the site itself does not set cookies or write to your browser's local storage.

Two third parties do run on the page: Google AdSense, which serves the advertising that pays for the site, and Google Analytics, which records aggregate page views. Both are Google services and both may use cookies or similar identifiers. The privacy policy and cookie policy set out exactly what they do and how to limit it.

How the site is funded

Hearth & Harvest is funded by advertising served through Google AdSense. There are no paid placements, no sponsored recipes and no affiliate links inside the instructions. Ads are served by Google, and their content is not chosen by this site. That advertising pays for hosting and for the time that goes into writing each page.

How the content is kept up to date

Recipes are reviewed when a reader reports a problem, when a method changes, or when an ingredient behaves differently from what the page describes. Corrections are made to the page itself and the sitemap is regenerated so search engines see the current version. If something on a recipe page does not work for you, the contact page is the place to say so, and specific details, such as the pan size or the oven setting you used, make a correction much easier to make.
