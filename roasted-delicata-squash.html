Roasted Delicata Squash with Sage

Prep Time: 10 mins Cook Time: 30 mins Servings: 4 Category: Healthy

Illustrative image.

Delicata is the one winter squash you can eat skin and all. The skin is thin enough to roast into something crisp, which means no peeling, no wrestling with a butternut, and a much shorter route to the oven.

It also roasts faster than other squash and holds its shape, so the slices come out with browned edges and a soft, custardy centre. Brown butter and fried sage are the classic pairing for a reason: the nuttiness of the butter meets the sweetness of the squash.

Ingredients

  • 2 medium delicata squash, about 1 1/2 lb total
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 12 fresh sage leaves
  • 3 tbsp unsalted butter
  • 1/3 cup toasted hazelnuts, roughly chopped
  • 1 tbsp maple syrup
  • 1/4 tsp ground cinnamon

Instructions

  1. Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C). Scrub the squash, cut it in half lengthwise and scoop out the seeds, then slice into half-moons about 3/4 inch thick. Delicata skin is thin enough to eat, so there is no peeling.
  2. Toss the slices with 2 tablespoons of the olive oil, the salt and pepper and spread them in a single layer on a sheet pan.
  3. Roast for 25 to 30 minutes, flipping once at 15 minutes, until the flesh is tender and the edges are browned and a little crisp.
  4. While the squash roasts, melt the butter in a small pan over medium heat. Add the sage leaves and fry for 60 to 90 seconds, until they darken and turn translucent at the edges. Take the pan off the heat and stir in the maple syrup and cinnamon.
  5. Tip the squash onto a platter, spoon the sage butter over it and scatter the hazelnuts on top. Serve warm; the skin softens as it cools, so this is best eaten soon after roasting.

Why this recipe works

The edible skin is what makes this work. It crisps against the pan while the flesh steams gently inside, giving two textures in one slice. Frying the sage in butter rather than sprinkling it raw mellows its resinous edge and infuses the butter, so every slice gets the flavour rather than just the leaves on top. The maple syrup goes in at the end because the squash is already sweet and sugar in the oven browns too fast.

Tips for success

  • Scrub rather than peel. The skin is the point, and any grit on it will end up on the plate.
  • Cut the halves into even half-moons and scoop the seeds with a spoon; the seed cavity is soft and easy to remove.
  • Use a pan large enough for a single layer. Overlapping slices steam and never brown.
  • Watch the sage closely. It goes from green to translucent to burnt in about thirty seconds.
  • Take the pan out of the oven a minute before you think it is ready; the edges keep darkening on the hot metal.

Substitutions that work

  • Acorn squash works but needs peeling and about ten extra minutes.
  • Pumpkin seeds or walnuts can replace the hazelnuts.
  • Olive oil can stand in for the butter, though you lose the brown butter flavour; add a pinch of salt to compensate.
  • Fresh thyme or rosemary can be fried in place of the sage, with a slightly different but equally good result.

Storage and make-ahead

Keep in the fridge for four days. Reheat at 400°F (200°C) for 8 minutes to bring the edges back, or eat it cold in a grain bowl, where the brown butter firms up and works as a dressing. The fried sage is best added fresh, as it softens in storage.

Serving suggestions

Serve alongside roast pork, chicken or a vegetarian grain dish. It also works as a starter with burrata and bread, or folded through warm farro with extra hazelnuts.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to peel delicata squash?

No. The skin is thin and edible, and it crisps beautifully in the oven. Just scrub it well before slicing.

Can I use a different squash?

Yes, but butternut and acorn need peeling and longer roasting, roughly 40 minutes at the same temperature.

Can I make this ahead?

Roast it up to a day ahead and reheat at 400°F (200°C) for 8 minutes. Fry the sage and melt the butter fresh just before serving.
