Chickpea Shakshuka

Prep Time: 10 mins Cook Time: 30 mins Servings: 4 Category: Breakfast

Illustrative image.

Shakshuka is eggs cooked in a pan of peppers and tomatoes, and it has the rare quality of being equally right at nine in the morning and at eight at night. The chickpeas turn what is essentially a sauce with eggs in it into something substantial enough to carry a meal.

The one thing that decides whether it works is the sauce. Under-cooked, it tastes of raw tinned tomato and the eggs slide around in it. Cooked down until it darkens and thickens, it holds the eggs in place and tastes sweet and deep.

Ingredients

  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 large onion, thinly sliced
  • 1 red bell pepper, thinly sliced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp sweet paprika
  • 1/2 tsp ground coriander
  • 1/4 tsp cayenne pepper
  • 1 can (28 oz) whole peeled tomatoes, crushed by hand
  • 1 can (15 oz) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 1/2 tsp sugar
  • 4 to 6 large eggs
  • 1/2 cup crumbled feta
  • 1/4 cup chopped parsley or cilantro
  • Crusty bread or warm flatbread, to serve

Instructions

  1. Warm the olive oil in a 12-inch skillet or shallow Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the onion and bell pepper with a pinch of salt and cook for 10 minutes, stirring now and then, until soft and beginning to colour at the edges.
  2. Add the garlic, cumin, paprika, coriander and cayenne and stir for a minute until fragrant. Tip in the crushed tomatoes, chickpeas, salt and sugar and simmer uncovered for 12 to 15 minutes, until the sauce has thickened and darkened and no longer tastes sharp.
  3. Make a small well in the sauce with the back of a spoon for each egg. Crack the eggs one at a time into a cup and slide each into its well, spacing them evenly around the pan.
  4. Cover the pan and cook over low heat for 8 to 12 minutes. The whites should be set and opaque while the yolks still wobble when the pan is nudged. Check at 8 minutes and keep the lid on longer only if the whites are still clear.
  5. Scatter the feta and herbs over the top, grind over a little black pepper and take the pan to the table with bread for scooping.

Why this recipe works

Cooking the tomato down properly is the difference between a stew and a pool of tomato juice. Twelve to fifteen minutes of uncovered simmering drives off water and concentrates the flavour, and the colour changes from bright orange-red to a darker brick. Adding the chickpeas before that reduction gives them time to absorb the sauce rather than sitting in it. The wells matter too: a deep hollow holds each egg so the white sets around the yolk instead of spreading into a thin sheet that overcooks.

Tips for success

  • Crush the tomatoes by hand rather than using diced. Diced tomatoes are firm enough to stay chunky and never break down into a sauce.
  • Season the sauce before the eggs go in. Once the eggs are added you cannot stir, so any salt missing at that stage stays missing.
  • Crack each egg into a small cup first. One broken yolk in the pan is hard to recover from.
  • Watch the whites, not the clock. They go from translucent to opaque somewhere between 8 and 12 minutes depending on the pan.
  • Take the pan off the heat when the whites are just set. They continue cooking in the residual heat while you carry it to the table.

Substitutions that work

  • Two roasted red peppers from a jar can replace the fresh one, added with the tomatoes instead of at the start.
  • Cannellini beans work in place of chickpeas and give a softer, creamier result.
  • Feta can be swapped for goat cheese, or left out for a dairy-free version, in which case add a little extra salt.
  • Smoked paprika in place of half the sweet paprika pushes it in a more robust direction.

Storage and make-ahead

The sauce alone keeps for four days in the fridge and freezes for three months, so it is worth doubling. Once eggs are cooked in it, it does not keep well: the whites toughen on reheating. If you have leftovers, fish out the eggs and reheat the sauce with fresh ones.

Serving suggestions

Warm flatbread or a torn baguette is the usual companion, and a spoon is all the equipment you need. A sharp cucumber salad cuts the richness.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make shakshuka without eggs?

Yes. Simmer the sauce with the chickpeas for an extra ten minutes and serve it as a stew over rice or with bread, or crumble in cubes of feta at the end.

Why is my shakshuka watery?

The sauce was not reduced enough before the eggs went in, or the tomatoes were diced rather than crushed. Simmer uncovered until a spoon dragged across the pan leaves a clear line.

Can I prepare the sauce ahead of time?

Yes, and it is the best way to serve this for a crowd. Make the sauce up to three days ahead, then reheat it in the pan and add the eggs just before serving.
