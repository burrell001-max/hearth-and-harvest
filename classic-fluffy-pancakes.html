Classic Fluffy Pancakes

Prep Time: 10 mins Cook Time: 15 mins Servings: 4 Category: Breakfast

Illustrative image.

Fluffy pancakes come down to a few small decisions: buttermilk for tenderness, a batter that is mixed just until it comes together, and a griddle that is hot enough to set the edges quickly. Do those three things and the pancakes rise into a proper stack.

The recipe makes about twelve pancakes, enough for four people. It is worth making the full batch even for two, because leftover pancakes freeze beautifully and reheat in the toaster.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 3 tbsp sugar
  • 2 tsp baking powder
  • 1 tsp baking soda
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 2 cups buttermilk (or whole milk with 1 tbsp lemon juice)
  • 1/4 cup unsalted butter, melted and cooled slightly
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • Extra butter or oil for the griddle
  • Maple syrup for serving

Instructions

  1. In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, sugar, baking powder, baking soda, and salt.
  2. In a separate medium bowl, whisk together the buttermilk, melted butter, eggs, and vanilla extract.
  3. Pour the wet ingredients into the dry ingredients. Use a wooden spoon or spatula to stir just until combined. *Tip: Do not overmix! A few lumps are completely fine and help keep the pancakes fluffy.* Let the batter rest for 5 minutes.
  4. Heat a large non-stick skillet or griddle over medium heat and lightly grease it with a little butter or oil.
  5. Scoop about 1/3 cup of batter onto the skillet for each pancake. Cook until bubbles begin to form on the surface and the edges look set (about 2-3 minutes).
  6. Carefully flip the pancake and cook the other side until golden brown (about another 1-2 minutes).
  7. Serve hot with a pat of butter and plenty of warm maple syrup!

Why this recipe works

Buttermilk reacts with baking soda to produce lift and a tender crumb, and its acidity keeps the pancakes from tasting flat. Letting the batter rest for five minutes hydrates the flour and lets the bubbles begin to form, which is why the second pancake is usually better than the first.

Tips for success

  • Mix the wet and dry ingredients separately, then combine with a few strokes. Lumps are fine; overmixing is not.
  • Let the batter rest for five minutes while the pan heats.
  • Flip when the surface is covered in bubbles and the edges look dry, and flip only once.

Substitutions that work

  • No buttermilk? Stir a tablespoon of lemon juice or vinegar into a cup of milk and let it stand for five minutes.
  • For a whole-grain version, replace half the flour with whole wheat pastry flour and add an extra splash of milk.
  • Melted coconut oil can replace the butter, giving a slightly different flavour but the same texture.

Storage and make-ahead

Cooled pancakes keep in the refrigerator for three days and freeze for two months with parchment between them. Reheat in a toaster or a 350 F oven for five minutes. The batter itself holds for a day in the fridge, though it will thicken.

Serving suggestions

Stack them with butter and warm maple syrup, or go savoury with a fried egg and bacon. Fresh berries, sliced banana or a spoonful of yogurt on top keeps the plate from being all sweetness.

Frequently asked questions

Why are my pancakes flat?

The batter was probably overmixed or the leavening was old. Stir until the flour just disappears, check the baking powder date, and do not press the pancakes with the spatula.

Can I make pancake batter the night before?

Yes. Keep it covered in the refrigerator and add a splash of milk in the morning, since the batter thickens overnight. Expect slightly less rise.

What is the best pan for pancakes?

A flat nonstick griddle or a well-seasoned cast iron pan holds an even temperature. Medium heat is usually right; too hot burns the outside before the middle sets.
