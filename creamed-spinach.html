Creamed Spinach

Prep Time: 10 mins Cook Time: 20 mins Servings: 4 Category: Dinner

Illustrative image.

Creamed spinach has a reputation for being a steakhouse side dish, but it is really just a white sauce with greens folded through it. Made at home with fresh spinach and a proper roux, it tastes of spinach first and cream second, which is not usually true of the frozen version.

It takes about half an hour and most of that is wilting and squeezing. The squeezing is not optional: spinach holds a surprising amount of water, and any that stays in the leaves will thin the sauce on the plate.

Ingredients

  • 2 lb fresh baby spinach
  • 3 tbsp unsalted butter
  • 1 small shallot, finely minced
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tbsp all-purpose flour
  • 1 cup whole milk
  • 1/2 cup heavy cream
  • 1/4 tsp freshly grated nutmeg
  • 3/4 tsp kosher salt
  • 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 1/3 cup grated Parmesan
  • 2 tbsp crisp fried shallots, to finish

Instructions

  1. Wilt the spinach in a large pot over medium heat with just the water clinging to the leaves, working in batches and turning with tongs. As soon as a batch collapses, move it to a colander. Press firmly with the back of a spoon to squeeze out the excess liquid, then chop roughly.
  2. Melt the butter in the same pot over medium-low heat and cook the shallot for 3 minutes until soft. Add the garlic and stir for 30 seconds.
  3. Sprinkle the flour over the shallots and cook, stirring, for 1 minute. Pour in the milk and cream in a steady stream, whisking, and simmer for 3 to 4 minutes until the sauce thickens enough to coat a spoon.
  4. Stir in the nutmeg, salt, pepper and Parmesan, then fold in the spinach. Cook for 2 minutes so the leaves heat through and absorb the sauce, then taste for salt.
  5. Serve in the pot with the fried shallots scattered over the top.

Why this recipe works

A roux made from equal parts butter and flour gives the sauce body without relying on reduced cream, so it stays stable and does not split. Cooking the flour for a full minute removes the raw taste that makes white sauces bland. Nutmeg is the traditional seasoning for spinach because its warmth lifts the mineral, slightly bitter edge of the leaves, and the Parmesan adds salt and savoury depth at the same time.

Tips for success

  • Wilt the spinach in batches with only the water left on the leaves after washing. Added water dilutes the flavour.
  • Squeeze hard. Wrap the cooled spinach in a clean tea towel and wring it out, or press it in a colander with a bowl on top.
  • Chop the squeezed spinach roughly so it folds through the sauce rather than forming long strings.
  • Whisk the milk in slowly and keep the heat moderate; a sauce that boils hard can turn grainy.
  • Add the Parmesan off the heat so it melts smoothly instead of clumping.

Substitutions that work

  • Frozen spinach works well if you thaw it and squeeze it thoroughly; use about 1 1/2 pounds.
  • Half-and-half can replace the cream, though the sauce will be thinner.
  • Gruyere in place of Parmesan gives a stronger, nuttier result.
  • For a dairy-free version, use olive oil and a plant milk with a little extra flour, though the texture will be different.

Storage and make-ahead

Keep covered in the fridge for three days. Reheat gently on the hob with a splash of milk, stirring, since the sauce thickens when cold. It does not freeze well: the cream separates and the spinach turns stringy on thawing.

Serving suggestions

Serve with roast beef, steak, roast chicken or a baked potato. It also makes a good filling for omelettes or a layer in a gratin, and a spoonful stirred through pasta with a little lemon zest is a quick dinner.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use frozen spinach?

Yes. Thaw 1 1/2 pounds, then squeeze it very thoroughly, as frozen spinach holds more water than fresh. The result is slightly softer but works well.

Why is my creamed spinach watery?

Either the leaves were not squeezed enough or the sauce was too thin to begin with. Squeeze the spinach in a towel and reduce the sauce until it coats a spoon before folding it in.

Can I make it ahead?

Make it up to two days ahead and reheat gently with a splash of milk. Add the fried shallots at the last minute so they stay crisp.
