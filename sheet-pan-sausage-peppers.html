Sheet-Pan Sausage and Peppers

Prep Time: 10 mins Cook Time: 35 mins Servings: 4 Category: Dinner

Illustrative image.

Sausages and peppers cooked together on one pan is the sort of dinner that feels like more effort went into it than actually did. The sausages brown in their own fat, the peppers soften underneath and catch the drippings, and the whole thing goes to the table on the pan it cooked on.

The version worth keeping is not the simmered one. High heat and a crowd-free pan give the peppers browned edges and the sausages a crackling skin, which is a different dish from the soft, stewed filling that turns up in deli trays.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 lb Italian sausage links, sweet or hot
  • 3 bell peppers in mixed colours, sliced into 1/2-inch strips
  • 2 medium red onions, cut into thick wedges
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 4 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 1/2 tsp fennel seeds, lightly crushed
  • 1/2 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • 1/4 cup torn fresh basil or chopped parsley

Instructions

  1. Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C) with a rack in the upper third. Line a large rimmed sheet pan with parchment or leave it bare if the pan is heavy and well seasoned.
  2. Put the peppers, onions, garlic, olive oil, oregano, fennel seeds, smoked paprika, salt and pepper on the pan and toss until every piece is slicked with oil. Spread everything into a single layer, leaving a little space between the strips so they roast rather than steam.
  3. Nestle the sausages among the vegetables, leaving gaps between them. Prick each sausage two or three times with the tip of a knife so the fat can escape and the casings do not split open.
  4. Roast for 30 to 35 minutes, turning the sausages over at the halfway point and giving the vegetables a stir and a shuffle back into an even layer. The sausages should read 160°F (71°C) at the centre and the pepper edges should be browned and slightly blistered.
  5. Pull the pan out, drizzle the vinegar over the hot vegetables and scatter the basil on top. Let everything sit for five minutes so the juices settle, then serve straight from the pan.

Why this recipe works

A hot oven does two jobs at once here. The dry heat crisps the sausage casing instead of leaving it flabby, which is what happens when sausages are simmered in sauce, and it drives moisture out of the peppers quickly enough that they brown before they collapse. Keeping the vegetables in strips rather than dice matters too: wide pieces hold their shape and still catch enough heat on the flat sides to char. The fennel seeds echo what is already in the sausage, and the vinegar stirred in at the end cuts through the fat without tasting sharp.

Tips for success

  • Dry the peppers thoroughly after washing. Surface water steams them in the first ten minutes, and once they have gone soft they never brown properly.
  • Prick the sausages in two or three places so the fat escapes and the casings do not split open under pressure.
  • Turn the sausages once at the halfway mark and stir the vegetables at the same time, scraping up the browned bits stuck to the pan.
  • Add the vinegar off the heat. Boiled vinegar loses the bright edge that makes the whole pan taste lighter.

Substitutions that work

  • Chicken sausages work but need less time: start checking at 20 minutes, since they dry out faster than pork.
  • A sliced fennel bulb in place of one pepper is excellent, and reinforces the fennel seed already in the dish.
  • Lemon juice or sherry vinegar will do the job of the red wine vinegar, though both need a little less.

Storage and make-ahead

Keep leftovers covered in the fridge for up to four days. Reheat uncovered at 375°F (190°C) for 10 to 12 minutes so the sausage skins tighten again; a microwave will leave them rubbery. The whole pan freezes well for up to three months, and it is worth freezing the vegetables and sausages together, since they reheat in the same time.

Serving suggestions

Spoon it over soft polenta, or pile it into a split roll with the juices soaked up by the bread. It also works on its own with a sharp green salad and a spoonful of mustard on the side, and any leftover peppers stirred through pasta the next day make a very good second dinner.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use raw sausage in this recipe?

Yes, provided they are fresh pork or chicken sausages and they reach 160°F (71°C) at the centre. Check with an instant-read thermometer in the thickest part rather than judging by colour.

Why did my peppers come out soft and grey?

Almost always crowding. If the vegetables are stacked more than one layer deep they steam in their own moisture instead of roasting, so use a second pan rather than piling them up.

Can I put this together the night before?

Yes. Toss the peppers, onions and seasonings and keep them covered in the fridge, then add the sausages and roast straight from cold, allowing five extra minutes.
