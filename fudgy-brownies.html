Fudgy Brownies

Prep Time: 15 mins Cook Time: 30 mins Servings: 9 Category: Dessert

Illustrative image.

These brownies are on the fudgy side of the line: dense, dark and a little gooey in the middle, with a thin shiny crust that cracks when you cut them. They are mixed in one bowl with a whisk and bake in thirty minutes.

The recipe makes nine generous squares in an eight-inch pan. Cocoa powder does most of the chocolate work, with chocolate chips folded in for pockets of melted chocolate rather than a uniform flavour.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, melted
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1/3 cup unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 tsp salt
  • 1/4 tsp baking powder
  • 1/2 cup semi-sweet chocolate chips

Instructions

  1. Preheat oven to 350°F (175°C). Line an 8-inch square baking pan with parchment paper, leaving an overhang on two sides.
  2. In a large bowl, whisk together the melted butter and sugar until smooth. Add the eggs and vanilla and whisk vigorously for about a minute, until the mixture lightens slightly in color.
  3. Sift in the cocoa powder, flour, salt, and baking powder. Fold gently with a spatula just until no dry streaks remain — take care not to overmix.
  4. Fold in the chocolate chips, then pour the batter into the prepared pan and spread evenly.
  5. Bake for 28-32 minutes, until a toothpick inserted in the center comes out with a few moist crumbs. Let cool completely in the pan before lifting out and slicing into squares.

Why this recipe works

A high ratio of sugar to flour keeps the crumb soft and fudgy, and melting the butter means the batter comes together without a mixer. Beating the eggs and sugar for a couple of minutes dissolves the sugar and gives the shiny top that people look for.

Tips for success

  • Line the pan with parchment, leaving an overhang, so you can lift the whole slab out and cut clean squares.
  • Beat the eggs and sugar until the mixture looks pale and slightly thickened, then fold in the dry ingredients.
  • Pull the pan when the centre is just set. A toothpick should come out with moist crumbs, not clean.

Substitutions that work

  • Dark chocolate chips or chopped chocolate can replace the semisweet for a less sweet, more intense brownie.
  • Melted coconut oil works in place of butter, though the brownies will be slightly less rich.
  • Add half a cup of walnut pieces or a teaspoon of espresso powder to deepen the chocolate flavour.

Storage and make-ahead

Store in an airtight container at room temperature for four days, or refrigerate for a week for a denser texture. They freeze well for three months, wrapped individually. Warm a square for ten seconds to bring back the gooey centre.

Serving suggestions

Serve with vanilla ice cream, a spoonful of salted caramel or a glass of cold milk. Cut small squares for a party, larger ones for a dessert with ice cream.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get a shiny top on brownies?

Beat the eggs and sugar thoroughly before adding the chocolate, and do not overbake. The shiny crust forms when the sugar dissolves and the surface dries before the centre sets.

Why are my brownies cakey instead of fudgy?

Too much flour, too much mixing or too long in the oven. Measure the flour carefully, fold gently, and pull the pan while the centre still looks slightly underdone.

Can I double the recipe?

Yes, but use two pans rather than one deep pan. A thicker slab needs a longer bake and the edges dry out before the middle sets.
