Cranberry Orange Sauce

Prep Time: 5 mins Cook Time: 15 mins Servings: 8 Category: Dinner

Cranberry sauce from a can is fine, but the homemade version takes fifteen minutes and tastes like actual fruit. Fresh cranberries pop in a saucepan with orange juice, sugar and a strip of zest, and the sauce thickens as it cools into a bright, tart spoonful.

This version leans on orange rather than spice, so it stays fresh-tasting next to rich food. It makes about two cups, enough for eight people at a holiday table with leftovers for sandwiches.

Ingredients

  • 12 oz (about 3 cups) fresh or frozen cranberries
  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 3/4 cup fresh orange juice
  • 1/4 cup water
  • Zest of 1 orange
  • 1 cinnamon stick (optional)
  • Pinch of salt

Instructions

  1. Combine the cranberries, sugar, orange juice, water, zest, cinnamon stick and salt in a medium saucepan.
  2. Bring to a boil over medium-high heat, stirring occasionally, then reduce to a steady simmer.
  3. Cook for 10 to 12 minutes, stirring now and then, until most of the berries have burst and the sauce looks glossy.
  4. Remove from the heat and discard the cinnamon stick. The sauce will look thin but thickens a lot as it cools.
  5. Cool to room temperature, then refrigerate until ready to serve.
  6. Do not overcook it. The pectin in cranberries sets the sauce as it cools.
  7. Stir in a splash of water if it is too thick after chilling.
  8. Taste once it is cool, since it will taste sweeter warm.
  9. A spoonful of orange liqueur or a squeeze of lemon at the end adds depth.
  10. Add 1/2 cup chopped pecans or walnuts just before serving.
  11. Stir in a teaspoon of fresh grated ginger while cooking.
  12. Replace half the water with apple cider for a deeper, fruitier flavor.

Why this recipe works

Cranberries are high in pectin, which means the sauce sets on its own as it cools. Cooking them just until most of the berries have burst keeps some texture and prevents the jammy, over-reduced result you get from a long simmer.

Tips for success

  • Rinse the cranberries and pick out any soft ones before cooking.
  • Stop cooking when about three quarters of the berries have popped. The rest will break down as you stir.
  • Taste after cooling, not while hot. The sauce tastes sweeter warm and sharpens considerably in the fridge.

Substitutions that work

  • Frozen cranberries work directly from frozen; add two or three minutes to the cooking time.
  • Honey can replace up to half the sugar, though it will darken the colour slightly.
  • A splash of port or orange liqueur stirred in off the heat adds depth for a grown-up version.

Storage and make-ahead

Keeps covered in the refrigerator for two weeks, and the flavour improves after a day. Freeze for up to three months in a sealed container. Serve cold or at room temperature; it does not need reheating.

Serving suggestions

Serve it with roast turkey, roast chicken or pork, and spread leftovers on a turkey sandwich with stuffing. It also works spooned over oatmeal or stirred into yogurt.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make cranberry sauce ahead of time?

Yes, and it is better made two or three days ahead so the flavours settle. Keep it covered in the refrigerator and bring it to room temperature before serving.

Why is my cranberry sauce too thick?

It was cooked too long or too much water evaporated. Stir in a spoonful or two of water or orange juice once it has cooled to loosen it.

Can I use dried cranberries?

No, they do not break down the same way. Fresh or frozen cranberries are needed for the sauce to set naturally.
