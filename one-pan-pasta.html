One-Pan Tomato & Basil Pasta

Prep Time: 5 mins Cook Time: 10 mins Servings: 4 Category: Quick Meals

One-pan pasta is the method where the spaghetti cooks directly in a shallow pan with the sauce ingredients and just enough water to cover. The starch that would normally go down the drain stays in the pan and thickens into a glossy sauce that coats every strand.

It sounds like a trick and it is, but it works. Four servings take about twenty minutes in one pan, with no colander and very little washing up.

Ingredients

  • 12 oz spaghetti
  • 1 bunch fresh basil
  • 1 large onion, thinly sliced
  • 4 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • 1 pint cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 4 1/2 cups water
  • 2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
  • 2 tsp salt
  • 1/4 tsp black pepper
  • Grated Parmesan cheese for serving

Instructions

  1. In a large, wide skillet or straight-sided pan, combine the spaghetti, basil, sliced onion, garlic, and halved cherry tomatoes.
  2. Pour the water and olive oil over the ingredients, and season everything with the salt and black pepper.
  3. Bring the liquids to a boil over high heat. Once boiling, use a pair of tongs to frequently turn and stir the pasta to keep it from sticking together.
  4. Cook for about 9 minutes, stirring continuously, until the pasta is perfectly al dente and the liquid has reduced into a rich, starchy sauce.
  5. Remove the pan from the heat, stir in any remaining fresh basil leaves, season to taste, and serve hot topped with plenty of grated Parmesan cheese.

Why this recipe works

Cooking the pasta in the sauce means it absorbs tomato and basil flavour from the inside out, and the released starch emulsifies with the olive oil to create body without cream. A wide pan is important because the pasta needs to lie mostly flat to cook evenly.

Tips for success

  • Use a straight-sided skillet wide enough for the spaghetti to lie nearly flat.
  • Keep the water at a steady boil and stir often so the pasta does not stick together.
  • Add the salt with the water rather than at the end, so it seasons the pasta as it cooks.

Substitutions that work

  • Linguine or bucatini work; short pasta shapes also work but need a little more water and stirring.
  • Cherry tomatoes are best, but chopped plum tomatoes will do in a pinch.
  • Add chili flakes, a parmesan rind or a handful of olives for a different flavour.

Storage and make-ahead

Best eaten immediately, as the pasta continues to absorb liquid as it sits. Leftovers keep for two days and can be loosened with a splash of water in a hot pan. Do not freeze.

Serving suggestions

Serve straight from the pan with grated parmesan, torn basil and a drizzle of olive oil. A green salad on the side is all it needs.

Why the pan shape matters

A wide, straight-sided skillet is essential here. The spaghetti needs to lie almost flat in a shallow layer of liquid so it cooks evenly, and a deep pot forces the strands to stand upright and clump together. Add the water first, then the pasta and the tomatoes, and bring it to a boil before turning the heat down to a strong simmer. Stir every minute or two, scraping the bottom, until the liquid has reduced to a glossy sauce. If the pasta is still firm when the water is nearly gone, add another half cup of hot water rather than turning up the heat.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my one-pan pasta turn out dry?

Not enough water, or the pan was too wide so it evaporated too fast. Add hot water as needed and keep the pan at a steady boil.

Can I use short pasta?

Yes, though it needs more water and closer attention. Penne and rigatoni take a few minutes longer than spaghetti.

Does the pasta really cook without draining?

Yes. The water reduces into a sauce as the pasta cooks, and the starch left behind is what thickens it.
