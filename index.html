How the recipe collection is organised

Every recipe here sits in one of five everyday categories: dinner, breakfast, dessert, healthy and quick meals. The chips above filter the grid as soon as you click them, and the search box matches words in the recipe titles and the short descriptions under each card, so typing pasta or lemon narrows the list immediately. Nothing reloads, and nothing you type is sent to a server.

The collection is deliberately broad rather than endless. There is one lasagna, one banana bread and one roast turkey breast, each written to be the version worth keeping rather than the fifth variation you have to compare. Most recipes use supermarket ingredients and equipment you probably already own: a skillet, a baking sheet, a saucepan and a box grater.

How a recipe page is laid out

Each page follows the same order: ingredients first, then numbered instructions, then notes on why the method works, substitutions that hold up, storage advice and the questions that tend to come up while cooking. Timings are written for a home oven, and where a step is easy to get wrong, the reason is explained instead of left as a warning.

If you are new here, the garlic and oil pasta is the fastest place to start, the chicken quesadillas are the most forgiving thing on the site, and the fudge chocolate cake is the one to make when there is something to celebrate. Breakfast cooks might prefer the fluffy pancakes.

Who writes the recipes
