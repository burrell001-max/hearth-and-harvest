Chicken Alfredo Pasta

Prep Time: 15 mins Cook Time: 20 mins Servings: 4 Category: Dinner

Illustrative image.

Real alfredo is not a jar of sauce poured over noodles. It is butter, cream, garlic and a serious amount of parmesan, cooked just long enough to come together and tossed with pasta that still has some bite.

This version adds seared chicken strips, which makes it a complete dinner in one pot and one pan. It takes about thirty-five minutes, and the sauce comes together in the time it takes the fettuccine to cook.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb fettuccine pasta
  • 2 large chicken breasts, sliced into strips
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter
  • 1 1/2 cups heavy whipping cream
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 1/2 cups freshly grated Parmesan cheese
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • Fresh parsley, chopped (for garnish)

Instructions

  1. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the fettuccine according to package directions until al dente. Drain and set aside.
  2. In a large skillet, heat the olive oil over medium-high heat. Season chicken strips with salt and pepper, then cook thoroughly until golden brown and cooked through (about 5-7 minutes). Remove chicken from skillet and set aside.
  3. Lower skillet heat to medium. Melt the butter, then add minced garlic and cook for 1 minute until fragrant.
  4. Pour in the heavy cream and let simmer gently for about 5 minutes, stirring frequently.
  5. Reduce heat to low and gradually stir in the grated Parmesan cheese until completely melted and smooth. Season the sauce with a pinch of salt and pepper.
  6. Toss the cooked pasta and chicken into the Alfredo sauce until completely coated. Garnish with chopped parsley and serve hot!

Why this recipe works

Freshly grated parmesan melts smoothly because it has not been coated with anti-caking agents, which is why pre-shredded cheese often turns grainy. Adding the cheese off the heat and stirring until it disappears keeps the sauce glossy, and a splash of pasta water loosens it without thinning the flavour.

Tips for success

  • Grate the parmesan finely on a microplane. Coarse shreds take longer to melt and can leave a gritty texture.
  • Pull the pan off the heat before adding the cheese and stir constantly until the sauce is smooth.
  • Reserve a cup of pasta water before draining. It is the best way to adjust the consistency at the end.

Substitutions that work

  • Half-and-half can replace the heavy cream for a lighter sauce, though it is more likely to split, so keep the heat low.
  • Penne, fettuccine, linguine or tagliatelle all work. Anything with a bit of surface area holds the sauce.
  • For a vegetarian version, leave out the chicken and add sautéed mushrooms or peas.

Storage and make-ahead

Alfredo does not reheat perfectly because the sauce thickens in the fridge. Store leftovers for up to two days and reheat gently with a splash of milk or water, stirring over low heat. Add the pasta to the sauce, not the other way around.

Serving suggestions

Serve immediately with extra parmesan, cracked pepper and a little chopped parsley. A crisp green salad cuts through the richness, and garlic bread is never a bad idea.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my alfredo sauce turn grainy?

The heat was too high or the parmesan was added too quickly. Take the pan off the burner, add the cheese in handfuls, and stir until each addition melts before adding more.

Can I make chicken alfredo without cream?

You can, using butter, pasta water and parmesan to build an emulsion, but the result is lighter and less stable than a cream-based sauce.

What is the best pasta for alfredo?

Fettuccine is traditional, but linguine, tagliatelle and penne all work well because the sauce clings to their surfaces.
