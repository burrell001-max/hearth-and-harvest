Caprese Grilled Cheese

Prep Time: 10 mins Cook Time: 10 mins Servings: 4 sandwiches Category: Quick Meals

A caprese grilled cheese is what happens when a summer salad meets a hot pan. Fresh mozzarella, sliced tomato and basil go between slices of sourdough, and the whole sandwich is cooked low and slow so the cheese melts before the bread scorches.

The trick is restraint. Too much tomato and the bread goes soggy; too much heat and the outside burns while the middle stays cold. Four sandwiches take about twenty minutes once the ingredients are sliced.

Ingredients

  • 8 slices sourdough or ciabatta
  • 8 oz fresh mozzarella, sliced
  • 2 ripe tomatoes, sliced
  • 1/4 cup fresh basil leaves
  • 2 tbsp basil pesto (optional)
  • 3 tbsp softened butter or olive oil
  • Salt and pepper
  • Balsamic glaze, for serving (optional)

Instructions

  1. Pat the tomato and mozzarella slices dry with paper towels and season the tomatoes lightly with salt and pepper.
  2. Spread pesto on the inside of 4 slices of bread. Layer mozzarella, tomato and basil, then top with the remaining bread.
  3. Butter the outside of each sandwich.
  4. Cook in a skillet over medium-low heat for 3 to 4 minutes per side, covered, until golden and the cheese is melted.
  5. Drizzle with balsamic glaze if you like, slice and serve.
  6. Medium-low heat lets the cheese melt before the bread burns.
  7. Covering the pan helps the cheese melt.
  8. Dry the tomatoes so the bread stays crisp.
  9. Add prosciutto or grilled chicken.
  10. Use mayonnaise on the outside of the bread for an extra-crisp crust.

Why this recipe works

Medium-low heat is the whole game. Mozzarella needs time to soften, and a hot pan will darken the bread long before the centre is molten. Patting the tomato slices dry removes the water that would otherwise soak into the crumb.

Tips for success

  • Dry the tomato and mozzarella slices with paper towels and season the tomatoes lightly with salt.
  • Spread a thin layer of pesto or butter on the inside of both slices to waterproof the bread a little.
  • Cover the pan with a lid for the first few minutes so the heat reaches the cheese from above as well as below.

Substitutions that work

  • Low-moisture mozzarella melts more evenly and makes a neater sandwich; fresh mozzarella tastes better but is wetter.
  • Ciabatta, country sourdough or a sturdy white loaf all work. Soft sliced bread tends to collapse under the filling.
  • Add prosciutto, grilled chicken or roasted red peppers if you want a more substantial sandwich.

Storage and make-ahead

Assemble and cook the sandwiches fresh; they do not keep well. If you must prepare them ahead, slice the components and store them separately, then build and cook just before serving.

Serving suggestions

Serve with a bowl of tomato soup for the obvious pairing, or a light green salad with a sharp vinaigrette. Cut diagonally and eat while the cheese is still pulling.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my grilled cheese burn before the cheese melted?

The pan was too hot. Use medium-low heat, cover the pan, and give the cheese five to eight minutes to soften gradually.

Can I make this in a panini press?

Yes. A press works well and speeds things up, though the sandwich will be flatter and the cheese will spread more evenly.

How do I keep the bread from getting soggy?

Dry the tomato slices well, use mozzarella that has been drained, and add a thin layer of pesto or butter on the inside of the bread to act as a barrier.
