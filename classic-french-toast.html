Classic Brioche French Toast

Prep Time: 10 mins Cook Time: 10 mins Servings: 4 Category: Breakfast

Illustrative image.

French toast is only as good as the bread. Thick slices of brioche or challah, a day or two old, soak up custard without falling apart, and the result is crisp at the edges with a soft, custardy middle.

This version keeps the custard simple, with cinnamon and vanilla, and cooks the slices in butter over medium heat. Eight slices serve four, and the whole thing takes twenty minutes.

Ingredients

  • 8 thick slices of day-old Brioche or Challah bread
  • 4 large eggs
  • 1 cup whole milk or heavy cream
  • 2 tbsp granulated sugar
  • 1 tbsp ground cinnamon
  • 2 tsp vanilla extract
  • Butter for the pan
  • Maple syrup and powdered sugar for serving

Instructions

  1. In a wide, shallow dish (like a pie plate), whisk together the eggs, milk, sugar, cinnamon, and vanilla extract until completely blended.
  2. Melt a tablespoon of butter in a large skillet or griddle over medium heat.
  3. Dip a slice of brioche bread into the egg mixture, allowing it to soak for about 10 seconds on each side. Lift up and let excess drip off.
  4. Place soaked bread onto hot skillet. Cook for 3-4 minutes per side until beautifully golden brown and slightly crisp on the edges. Repeat for all slices, serving hot with syrup.

Why this recipe works

A shallow dish wide enough for a whole slice lets the bread soak evenly, and a ten-second soak per side is enough for stale brioche. Cooking over medium rather than high heat gives the custard time to set before the butter browns, so the centre is cooked rather than raw.

Tips for success

  • Use bread that is a day or two old. Fresh brioche falls apart in the custard.
  • Soak each slice briefly and let the excess drip back into the dish.
  • Wipe the pan between batches and add fresh butter so the second batch does not taste burnt.

Substitutions that work

  • Challah, Portuguese sweet bread or a sturdy country loaf all work in place of brioche.
  • Whole milk makes a lighter custard; heavy cream makes it richer and more decadent.
  • For a dairy-free version, use oat milk and cook in a neutral oil instead of butter.

Storage and make-ahead

French toast is best straight from the pan. Cooked slices can be held in a 200 F oven for twenty minutes if you are feeding a crowd. Leftovers keep for two days and reheat well in a toaster.

Serving suggestions

Serve with maple syrup, a knob of butter and fresh berries, or with bacon on the side for a savoury contrast. Powdered sugar is traditional but optional.

Choosing and cutting the bread

Ask for a whole brioche or challah loaf rather than a pre-sliced one, and cut it yourself into slices about an inch thick. Thick slices hold custard in the middle while the outside crisps, which is the whole point of the dish. If the loaf is very fresh, spread the slices on a tray and leave them uncovered for an hour, or toast them lightly before dipping. Do not press the bread down into the custard with a spatula; let it soak up what it needs and lift it out gently. A dry slice will float, and a saturated one will fall apart.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my French toast soggy in the middle?

The bread was soaked too long or the heat was too high, browning the outside before the custard set. Use stale bread, soak briefly and cook over medium heat.

Can I use regular sandwich bread?

You can, but it is too thin and soft to hold much custard. If that is all you have, use two slices pressed together and cook them gently.

What is the difference between French toast and French toast casserole?

Casserole is baked in a dish with cubed bread and a custard poured over it, which suits feeding a crowd. French toast is cooked slice by slice in a pan.
