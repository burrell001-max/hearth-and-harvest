Classic Pumpkin Pie

Prep Time: 20 mins Cook Time: 65 mins Servings: 8 Category: Dessert

Pumpkin pie is a custard pie, and like all custards it rewards a little patience. The filling is mixed in one bowl, poured into a deep-dish crust and baked until the edges are set and the centre still moves like a soft set jelly.

This recipe uses canned pumpkin puree, evaporated milk and the usual warm spices. It serves eight and is best made a day ahead, which is convenient when the oven is already busy.

Ingredients

  • 1 unbaked 9-inch deep-dish pie crust
  • 1 can (15 oz) pure pumpkin puree (not pumpkin pie filling)
  • 1 can (12 fl oz) evaporated milk
  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 tsp ground ginger
  • 1/4 tsp ground cloves
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • Whipped cream, for serving

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 425°F (220°C) and place the unbaked crust in a 9-inch pie plate.
  2. In a large bowl, whisk the sugar, cinnamon, ginger, cloves and salt. Add the eggs and beat lightly.
  3. Stir in the pumpkin puree, then gradually whisk in the evaporated milk until smooth.
  4. Pour the filling into the crust. Bake for 15 minutes at 425°F.
  5. Reduce the oven to 350°F (175°C) and bake for 40 to 50 minutes more, until a knife inserted near the center comes out clean. The center may still jiggle slightly.
  6. Cool the pie on a wire rack for at least 2 hours, then refrigerate until serving. Serve with whipped cream.
  7. Do not overbake. A slightly wobbly center sets as it cools, and overbaking causes cracks.
  8. If the crust edge browns too fast, cover it loosely with foil or a pie shield.
  9. Cool the pie slowly at room temperature before chilling to help prevent cracking.
  10. Place the pie plate on a baking sheet to catch drips and make it easier to move.
  11. Top with a pecan streusel or maple whipped cream.
  12. Use a gingersnap cookie crust for extra spice.
  13. Bake in individual ramekins without a crust for a crustless pumpkin custard.

Why this recipe works

Evaporated milk gives the filling body without the extra water of fresh milk, so it sets reliably. Pulling the pie while the centre is still slightly wobbly is the key step; carry-over heat finishes the cooking as it cools, and stopping there is what keeps the surface from cracking.

Tips for success

  • Use pure pumpkin puree, not pumpkin pie filling, which is already sweetened and spiced.
  • Whisk the eggs in one at a time so the filling stays smooth and does not incorporate air.
  • Cool the pie completely at room temperature before refrigerating, to reduce cracking.

Substitutions that work

  • A graham cracker or gingersnap crust works in place of pastry and needs no blind baking.
  • Half-and-half can replace the evaporated milk, though the filling will take longer to set.
  • For a dairy-free version, use full-fat coconut milk and expect a slightly softer set.

Storage and make-ahead

Covered and refrigerated, pumpkin pie keeps for four days. It also freezes well for up to a month; thaw overnight in the refrigerator. Serve it cold or at cool room temperature, not warm, so the filling holds its shape.

Serving suggestions

Top with lightly sweetened whipped cream and a dusting of cinnamon. It belongs on a Thanksgiving table next to stuffing and cranberry sauce, but it is just as good with a cup of coffee on a quiet afternoon.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know when pumpkin pie is done?

The outer two inches should look set and the centre should still wobble slightly when you nudge the pan. It firms up as it cools.

Why did my pumpkin pie crack?

Usually it was overbaked or cooled too quickly. Pull it while the centre is still loose, cool it gradually and avoid a cold draught.

Can I use fresh pumpkin instead of canned?

Yes, but cook and drain it well first. Fresh pumpkin holds more water, so the filling may need a little longer in the oven.
