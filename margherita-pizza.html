Margherita Pizza

Prep Time: 20 mins (+ dough resting) Cook Time: 10 mins Servings: 2 pizzas Category: Dinner

Margherita pizza is proof that four ingredients can be enough. Dough, tomato sauce, fresh mozzarella and basil, with a little olive oil and salt, produce something better than most delivery pizzas if the oven is properly hot and the dough is not overloaded.

This method uses a pizza stone or an inverted baking sheet preheated for at least half an hour, which is the closest most home ovens get to a pizza oven. Two pizzas serve two people generously.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb pizza dough, store-bought or homemade, room temperature
  • 1/2 cup San Marzano tomato sauce, lightly seasoned with salt
  • 8 oz fresh mozzarella, torn into pieces
  • Handful fresh basil leaves
  • 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
  • Flaky sea salt, to finish
  • Semolina or flour, for dusting

Instructions

  1. Preheat your oven as hot as it will go (500°F/260°C or higher) with a pizza stone or inverted baking sheet inside for at least 30 minutes.
  2. On a floured surface, stretch the dough into a 10-12 inch round, keeping the edges slightly thicker for the crust.
  3. Transfer the dough to a pizza peel or parchment-lined baking sheet dusted with semolina.
  4. Spread the tomato sauce thinly over the dough, leaving a border for the crust.
  5. Scatter torn mozzarella evenly over the sauce.
  6. Slide the pizza onto the hot stone (or place the baking sheet in the oven) and bake for 8-10 minutes, until the crust is blistered and golden and the cheese is bubbling.
  7. Remove from the oven, top with fresh basil leaves, drizzle with olive oil, and finish with a pinch of flaky sea salt before slicing.

Why this recipe works

Preheating the stone for a long time stores heat that transfers into the base immediately, which is what produces a blistered, crisp crust rather than a pale one. Tearing the mozzarella and patting it dry stops the pizza turning into a puddle of milky water in the middle.

Tips for success

  • Bring the dough to room temperature and stretch it with your hands rather than a rolling pin, which crushes the gas out of the edge.
  • Spread the sauce thinly. A heavy hand makes the middle soggy.
  • Add the basil after baking, or in the last minute, so it does not blacken.

Substitutions that work

  • Store-bought dough is fine; look for one sold fresh rather than frozen if possible.
  • Low-moisture mozzarella melts more predictably than fresh, though fresh tastes better.
  • A cast iron skillet works as a pizza pan if you do not have a stone, preheated on the stovetop first.

Storage and make-ahead

Pizza is best straight from the oven. Leftover slices keep for two days and reheat best in a dry skillet over medium heat for three minutes, which crisps the base again. The microwave leaves it floppy.

Serving suggestions

Serve with a drizzle of olive oil, extra basil and a pinch of flaky salt. A simple arugula salad with lemon and parmesan is enough on the side.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my pizza crust soggy in the middle?

Too much sauce, wet mozzarella or an oven that is not hot enough. Drain the mozzarella, sauce lightly and preheat the stone for at least thirty minutes.

Can I make pizza without a stone?

Yes. Use an inverted heavy baking sheet preheated in the oven, or bake the pizza in a preheated cast iron skillet.

How hot should the oven be for pizza?

As hot as it will go, usually 500 to 550 F. A hotter oven gives a crisper base and better blisters on the crust.
