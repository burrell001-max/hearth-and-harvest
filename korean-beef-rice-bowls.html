Korean-Style Beef Rice Bowls

Prep Time: 15 mins Cook Time: 12 mins Servings: 4 Category: Quick Meals

Illustrative image.

This is the dinner to make when the fridge is nearly empty and nobody wants to think. Ground beef, a sauce built from things that keep in the door of the fridge, and rice. Twenty-five minutes, one pan, and a bowl that tastes considerably more considered than the effort involved.

The detail that lifts it above plain seasoned mince is the glaze. Soy, sugar, sesame and gochujang cooked down until they cling to the meat give the same sticky, savoury finish that makes the restaurant version so hard to stop eating.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb ground beef, 85/15
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp freshly grated ginger
  • 1/4 cup soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp brown sugar
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil
  • 1 tbsp gochujang, or to taste
  • 1 tsp cornstarch mixed with 2 tbsp cold water
  • 4 cups hot cooked short-grain rice
  • 1 Persian cucumber, thinly sliced
  • 1 carrot, cut into thin ribbons
  • 2 tbsp rice vinegar
  • 1 tsp sesame seeds
  • 3 scallions, thinly sliced

Instructions

  1. Stir the cucumber and carrot with the rice vinegar and a pinch of salt and set aside. They will soften and pickle slightly while the beef cooks.
  2. Heat a large skillet over high heat until a drop of water skitters across the surface. Add the beef and press it into an even layer. Leave it alone for 3 minutes so a browned crust forms before you break it up.
  3. Add the garlic and ginger and cook for 30 seconds, then stir in the soy sauce, brown sugar, sesame oil and gochujang. Simmer for 2 minutes until the sauce coats the meat.
  4. Stir the cornstarch slurry and add it to the pan. Cook for 30 to 60 seconds, until the sauce turns glossy and clings rather than pooling.
  5. Spoon the beef over the hot rice, add the pickled vegetables and finish with sesame seeds and scallions.

Why this recipe works

Getting the pan properly hot and leaving the beef alone for the first few minutes builds a browned crust that seasoned mince never gets when it is stirred from the start. The cornstarch slurry is what turns a watery sauce into a glaze: it thickens the liquid so it coats each grain of rice instead of running to the bottom of the bowl. The quick pickle is not decoration either, since the vinegar and crunch cut through the sweetness of the beef.

Tips for success

  • Heat the pan until a drop of water skitters across it before the beef goes in. A lukewarm pan gives you grey mince.
  • Press the beef flat and leave it for three minutes. That pause is where the browning happens.
  • Mix the cornstarch with cold water just before adding it. Left standing, it settles into a hard layer at the bottom of the cup.
  • Taste the sauce before it goes in. Gochujang brands vary a lot in heat and sweetness, so adjust the sugar and chili to what you have.
  • Slice the cucumber and carrot thinly, ideally on a mandoline. Thick pieces stay raw-tasting rather than lightly pickled.

Substitutions that work

  • Ground turkey or pork works well; pork is closer in fat content and browns more easily.
  • Honey or maple syrup can replace the brown sugar, though both are sweeter, so use a little less.
  • No gochujang? Sriracha with a teaspoon of miso gets you close, or use chili garlic sauce and add a pinch of sugar.
  • Cauliflower rice stands in for the rice if you want it lighter, but drain it well or the bowl turns soupy.

Storage and make-ahead

The beef keeps for three days in the fridge and freezes for two months; reheat in a hot pan with a spoonful of water to loosen the glaze. Cooked rice should be cooled quickly and refrigerated within an hour, then used within a day. Keep the pickled vegetables separate so they stay crisp.

Serving suggestions

Serve in deep bowls with the rice hot and the toppings cold, and add a fried egg on top if you want it more filling. Steamed broccoli or wilted spinach slipped in at the side makes it a complete meal, and a drizzle of extra sesame oil at the table is never a mistake.

Frequently asked questions

Is gochujang very hot?

Less hot than most people expect. It is fermented and slightly sweet, closer to a mild sriracha in heat than to a chili paste, though brands differ. Start with one tablespoon and taste.

Can I use ground turkey instead of beef?

Yes. Use turkey thigh rather than breast if you can, add an extra tablespoon of oil to the pan, and brown it a minute longer, since leaner meat colours more slowly.

What rice works best for these bowls?

Short-grain or sushi rice, because it clumps slightly and holds the sauce. Jasmine rice is fine, but rinse it well first so the grains stay separate.
