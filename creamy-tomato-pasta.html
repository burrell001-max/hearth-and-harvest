Creamy Tomato Pasta

Prep Time: 10 mins Cook Time: 20 mins Servings: 4 Category: Dinner

Creamy tomato pasta is a weeknight compromise that does not taste like one. A quick sauce of onion, garlic, crushed tomatoes and tomato paste cooks in the time it takes the pasta to boil, and a small amount of cream turns it velvety without drowning the tomato.

It is worth using good canned tomatoes here, because there are only a handful of ingredients and nothing to hide behind. The recipe serves four with enough sauce to coat every piece of pasta.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb penne or rigatoni pasta
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 small yellow onion, finely diced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 can (14 oz) crushed tomatoes
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1/2 cup heavy cream
  • 1/2 cup grated Parmesan cheese
  • 1/2 tsp dried oregano
  • A handful of fresh basil leaves, torn
  • Salt and crushed red pepper flakes to taste

Instructions

  1. Cook the pasta in a large pot of boiling salted water until al dente. Reserve 1/2 cup of pasta water before draining.
  2. Heat olive oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Add the diced onion and cook until translucent and soft (about 5 minutes). Add minced garlic and tomato paste, cooking for another 1-2 minutes until fragrant.
  3. Pour in the crushed tomatoes, dried oregano, salt, and a pinch of red pepper flakes. Reduce heat to low and let it simmer gently for 10 minutes to develop the flavors.
  4. Stir the heavy cream into the tomato sauce, blending until it turns a rich, velvety orange color. Let it warm through for 2 minutes.
  5. Add the drained pasta and the grated Parmesan cheese directly into the skillet. Toss everything together over low heat until the sauce coats the pasta beautifully. (If the sauce is too thick, splash in a little bit of the reserved pasta water).
  6. Remove from heat, stir in the fresh basil, and serve immediately with extra Parmesan on top.

Why this recipe works

Tomato paste deepens the colour and flavour in a way that a longer simmer would normally provide. Adding the cream off a hard boil keeps it from splitting, and finishing the pasta in the sauce with a splash of pasta water makes the sauce cling instead of pooling at the bottom of the bowl.

Tips for success

  • Cook the onion until translucent before adding the garlic, so the garlic does not burn and turn bitter.
  • Reserve a cup of pasta water before draining; it is what brings the sauce together at the end.
  • Add the cream at the very end, off the heat, and stir rather than boil.

Substitutions that work

  • Whole peeled tomatoes crushed by hand give a more rustic texture than pre-crushed tomatoes.
  • Half-and-half or whole milk can replace the cream, though the sauce will be thinner and needs a minute longer to reduce.
  • Penne, rigatoni or fusilli all hold this sauce well. Long pasta works too, but toss it thoroughly.

Storage and make-ahead

Refrigerate leftovers for three days. Reheat gently on the stove with a splash of water, stirring until the sauce loosens. The pasta will absorb sauce overnight, so it may need more liquid than you expect.

Serving suggestions

Finish with torn basil, grated parmesan and a grind of black pepper. A piece of garlic bread and a green salad turn it into a full dinner.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make creamy tomato pasta without cream?

Yes. Use a tablespoon of butter and a little pasta water, or a spoonful of mascarpone, to give the sauce body without a full cream base.

Why did my sauce taste bitter?

The garlic probably burned, or the tomatoes were undercooked. Cook the garlic gently for a minute only, and let the sauce simmer for at least ten minutes.

Can I add protein?

Yes. Sliced chicken, shrimp, sausage or white beans all work. Brown them first and stir them in at the end so they do not overcook in the sauce.
