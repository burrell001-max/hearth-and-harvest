Herb Roasted Turkey Breast

Prep Time: 20 mins Cook Time: 120 mins Total: 140 mins Servings: 8

A bone-in turkey breast gives you all the Thanksgiving flavor of a whole bird without the size or the effort. This version is rubbed with garlic and herb butter, which seasons the meat and crisps the skin.

It is a good choice for a smaller gathering, and it cooks much faster than a whole turkey.

Ingredients

  • 1 bone-in turkey breast (4 to 5 lb), thawed
  • 4 tbsp (1/2 stick) unsalted butter, softened
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp chopped fresh rosemary
  • 1 tbsp chopped fresh thyme
  • 1 tbsp chopped fresh sage
  • 1 1/2 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 1 cup chicken broth
  • 1 onion, quartered

Instructions

  1. Take the turkey breast out of the refrigerator 30 minutes before roasting. Preheat the oven to 325°F (165°C).
  2. Mix the butter, garlic, herbs, salt and pepper. Pat the turkey dry and rub half of the butter under the skin and the rest over the top.
  3. Place the onion and broth in a roasting pan, set the turkey on top, skin side up.
  4. Roast for about 1 3/4 to 2 1/2 hours, until the thickest part reaches 165°F (74°C) on an instant-read thermometer. Tent with foil if the skin gets too dark.
  5. Rest, covered loosely, for 20 minutes before slicing. Use the pan juices for gravy.

Ingredient Notes and Substitutions

Turkey

Make sure it is fully thawed. Allow about 24 hours in the refrigerator for every 4 to 5 pounds.

Herbs

Fresh herbs have the best flavor. If you use dried, use about one third as much.

Tips for Success

  • Cook to temperature, not time. Ovens and breasts vary.
  • Do not skip the rest. It keeps the meat juicy.
  • Check the thickest part without touching the bone.

Variations

  • Add lemon zest to the butter.
  • Brine the turkey for 8 to 12 hours first for extra juiciness.

Storage and Make-Ahead

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days. Freeze sliced turkey with some broth for up to 2 months.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should I roast it?

Roughly 20 to 25 minutes per pound, but go by a thermometer reading of 165°F.

Should I cover it?

Tent loosely with foil if the skin is getting too dark.

Can I roast it from frozen?

I do not recommend it. Thaw it fully first.
