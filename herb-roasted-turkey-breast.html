Herb Roasted Turkey Breast

Prep Time: 20 mins Cook Time: 120 mins Servings: 8 Category: Dinner

A whole turkey is a lot of bird for most gatherings. A bone-in breast gives you the same roast-dinner centrepiece in a fraction of the time, and it is far easier to keep juicy because there is less meat to overcook.

This one is rubbed with garlic herb butter, roasted over onion and broth, and pulled at 160 F so it carries over to a safe, moist 165 F while it rests. Four to five pounds serve eight.

Ingredients

  • 1 bone-in turkey breast (4 to 5 lb), thawed
  • 4 tbsp (1/2 stick) unsalted butter, softened
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp chopped fresh rosemary
  • 1 tbsp chopped fresh thyme
  • 1 tbsp chopped fresh sage
  • 1 1/2 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 1 cup chicken broth
  • 1 onion, quartered

Instructions

  1. Take the turkey breast out of the refrigerator 30 minutes before roasting. Preheat the oven to 325°F (165°C).
  2. Mix the butter, garlic, herbs, salt and pepper. Pat the turkey dry and rub half of the butter under the skin and the rest over the top.
  3. Place the onion and broth in a roasting pan, set the turkey on top, skin side up.
  4. Roast for about 1 3/4 to 2 1/2 hours, until the thickest part reaches 165°F (74°C) on an instant-read thermometer. Tent with foil if the skin gets too dark.
  5. Rest, covered loosely, for 20 minutes before slicing. Use the pan juices for gravy.
  6. Cook to temperature, not time. Ovens and breasts vary.
  7. Do not skip the rest. It keeps the meat juicy.
  8. Check the thickest part without touching the bone.
  9. Add lemon zest to the butter.
  10. Brine the turkey for 8 to 12 hours first for extra juiciness.

Why this recipe works

The bone insulates the meat and conducts heat more gently than a boneless roast, which gives a wider window between perfectly cooked and dry. Resting for twenty minutes lets the juices redistribute; slicing immediately is what leaves a puddle of juice on the board and dry meat on the plate.

Tips for success

  • Take the breast out of the refrigerator thirty minutes before roasting so it cooks evenly.
  • Loosen the skin and rub the herb butter underneath as well as on top.
  • Use an instant-read thermometer in the thickest part, away from the bone, and pull it at 160 F.

Substitutions that work

  • A boneless breast works but needs a shorter roast; start checking at 45 minutes.
  • Dried herbs are fine: use a teaspoon each of dried rosemary, thyme and sage.
  • Add lemon zest to the butter, or brine the breast for eight hours for extra juiciness.

Storage and make-ahead

Refrigerate sliced turkey for three days, covered, with a little broth to keep it moist. Reheat gently at 300 F covered with foil. Leftovers make excellent sandwiches, soup and pot pie, and the carcass makes a good stock.

Serving suggestions

Serve with mashed potatoes, sage stuffing, green bean casserole and cranberry sauce. A spoonful of the pan juices over the sliced meat is worth the effort.

Frequently asked questions

What temperature should turkey breast be cooked to?

165 F in the thickest part, away from the bone. Pulling it at 160 F and letting it rest delivers that temperature without drying the meat out.

How long does a 4 to 5 pound turkey breast take?

About two hours at 325 F, depending on the shape of the roast and whether it is bone-in. Start checking at 90 minutes with a thermometer.

Should I cover the turkey with foil?

Tent it loosely if the skin is browning too quickly. Covering it for the whole roast steams the skin and you lose the crispness.
