Farro and Roasted Vegetable Bowl

Prep Time: 15 mins Cook Time: 35 mins Servings: 4 Category: Healthy

Grain bowls fail in a predictable way: everything is soft, everything is the same temperature, and the dressing is the only thing you can taste. This one is built the other way round, with chewy farro, vegetables roasted until the edges are dark, and cold, sharp elements added at the end.

It is also the most useful thing in this collection to make on a Sunday. The components keep separately for days and assemble in three minutes, which is a better lunch than anything within walking distance of most offices.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup pearled farro, rinsed
  • 3 cups low-sodium vegetable stock
  • 1 small butternut squash, peeled and cut into 3/4-inch cubes
  • 1 head broccoli, cut into small florets
  • 1 red onion, cut into wedges
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1/2 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 1/3 cup tahini
  • 2 tbsp lemon juice
  • 1 clove garlic, grated
  • 1/4 cup toasted pepitas
  • 1/3 cup crumbled feta
  • 1/4 cup chopped flat-leaf parsley

Instructions

  1. Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C). Toss the squash, broccoli and onion with the olive oil, cumin, paprika and salt and spread them across two sheet pans, keeping the broccoli on its own pan so it does not steam under the squash.
  2. Roast for 30 to 35 minutes, turning everything once at 20 minutes. The squash should be tender enough to pierce with a knife without resistance and the broccoli tips should be dark at the edges.
  3. While the vegetables roast, bring the stock to a boil in a saucepan, add the farro and simmer uncovered for 20 to 25 minutes until the grains are chewy but no longer chalky in the centre. Drain off any liquid that remains.
  4. Whisk the tahini with the lemon juice, garlic, a pinch of salt and 3 to 4 tablespoons of cold water. It will seize and thicken at first; keep adding water a spoonful at a time until it pours in a slow ribbon.
  5. Divide the farro between four bowls, pile the roasted vegetables on top and spoon the dressing over. Finish with the pepitas, feta and parsley.

Why this recipe works

Farro has a firm, almost springy texture that survives being dressed and reheated, which rice does not. Roasting the broccoli on its own pan keeps it from sitting under the squash, where it would steam and lose the browned tips that give the bowl its savoury note. The tahini dressing is deliberately loose: tahini thickens sharply when it meets lemon, so whipping in cold water brings it back to a pourable consistency that coats rather than clumps.

Tips for success

  • Rinse the farro before cooking. Pearled farro still carries surface starch that makes the water gluey.
  • Salt the cooking water properly, as you would pasta. Farro absorbs salt only while it cooks.
  • Cut the squash into even cubes, no larger than three-quarters of an inch, or the smaller pieces will collapse before the larger ones are done.
  • Roast the vegetables on two pans. One crowded pan is the most common reason a grain bowl tastes bland.
  • Dress the farro while it is still warm, so it absorbs the dressing instead of sitting under it.

Substitutions that work

  • Brown rice, barley or wheat berries all work in place of farro, with their own cooking times.
  • Sweet potato or cauliflower can replace the squash, and both roast in roughly the same time.
  • Greek yogurt thinned with lemon juice makes a good stand-in for the tahini dressing.
  • Skip the feta and add extra pepitas for a dairy-free version, plus a pinch more salt.

Storage and make-ahead

Keep the farro, vegetables and dressing in separate containers for up to four days. Reheat the grains and vegetables together in a pan or microwave, then add the dressing, feta and seeds cold. Dressed bowls keep for a day only, since the farro soaks up everything and the greens go limp.

Serving suggestions

Eat it warm for dinner, or cold from the fridge as a lunch. A handful of rocket or spinach under the grains adds freshness, and a squeeze of lemon over the top just before eating restores the edge the dressing loses overnight.

Frequently asked questions

Is farro gluten-free?

No. Farro is a wheat grain and contains gluten, so use quinoa, millet or buckwheat for a gluten-free bowl, adjusting the cooking time to about 15 minutes.

Can I cook farro ahead of time?

Yes, and it is better that way. Cook it, drain it, toss with a little olive oil and keep it in the fridge for up to four days. It reheats in a minute in a pan.

Why did my tahini dressing turn thick and grainy?

That is normal: tahini seizes when acid hits it. Keep adding cold water a spoonful at a time while whisking and it will loosen into a smooth, pourable dressing.
