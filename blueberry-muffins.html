Blueberry Muffins

Prep Time: 15 mins Cook Time: 20 mins Servings: 12 muffins Category: Breakfast

The best blueberry muffins are barely sweet, heavy with fruit and crowned with a tall, slightly crackled dome. That dome comes from a hot oven and a batter thick enough to hold the berries in place rather than letting them sink to the bottom.

These muffins use melted butter and a simple stir-together method, so they take about fifteen minutes to prepare. A dozen muffins disappear quickly, which is worth remembering when you buy blueberries.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar, plus extra for topping
  • 2 tsp baking powder
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, melted
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1/2 cup milk
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 1/2 cups fresh blueberries

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 400°F (200°C) and line a 12-cup muffin tin with paper liners.
  2. In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt.
  3. In a separate bowl, whisk together the melted butter, eggs, milk, and vanilla extract.
  4. Pour the wet ingredients into the dry ingredients and stir until just combined.
  5. Gently fold in the blueberries, being careful not to overmix.
  6. Divide the batter evenly among the muffin cups, filling each about 3/4 full. Sprinkle a little sugar on top of each.
  7. Bake for 18-20 minutes, until golden and a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean.
  8. Cool in the tin for 5 minutes before transferring to a wire rack.

Why this recipe works

Starting the oven at 400 F gives the batter an initial burst of heat that sets the edges and lifts the tops before the centres finish cooking. Melting the butter avoids the need for a mixer, and a thick batter, made with a full two cups of flour, suspends the berries instead of letting them drop.

Tips for success

  • Toss the blueberries with a spoonful of the measured flour before folding them in. The coating keeps them from sinking.
  • Fill the liners almost to the top. A generous fill is what produces a proper bakery-style dome.
  • Sprinkle the tops with a pinch of sugar before baking for a thin, crunchy crust.

Substitutions that work

  • Frozen blueberries work if you do not thaw them. Expect a slightly wetter batter and a few extra minutes in the oven.
  • Whole milk or buttermilk can replace the milk one for one; buttermilk gives a tangier, more tender crumb.
  • Swap a quarter cup of the flour for almond flour for a slightly denser, nuttier muffin.

Storage and make-ahead

Keep muffins in an airtight container at room temperature for two days, then refrigerate for another two. Reheat for ten seconds in the microwave to soften them. They freeze well for up to three months; thaw at room temperature.

Serving suggestions

Eat them warm with butter, or split and toast them under the grill. They make a good addition to a breakfast spread alongside a berry baked oatmeal or a bowl of yogurt and fruit.

Frequently asked questions

Why do my blueberries sink to the bottom?

The batter is usually too thin or the berries too heavy and wet. Toss the berries in flour, keep the batter thick, and do not overmix it.

Can I use frozen blueberries?

Yes. Add them frozen and unthawed so they do not bleed through the batter, and allow a couple of extra minutes of baking time.

How do I get tall muffin tops?

Preheat the oven fully, fill the liners nearly to the top, and bake at a higher temperature for the first part of the bake so the edges set quickly.
