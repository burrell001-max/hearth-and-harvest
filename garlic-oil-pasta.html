Garlic & Oil Pasta

Prep Time: 10 mins Cook Time: 15 mins Servings: 2 Category: Dinner

Garlic and oil pasta, or aglio e olio, is the dish to make when the fridge is empty and dinner still needs to be good. It uses eight ingredients, most of which you already have, and the technique is entirely about the garlic.

The garlic is sliced thin and cooked gently in olive oil until it is pale gold. Take it too far and it turns bitter, which is the only real way to ruin this dish. Two generous servings take about twenty-five minutes.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 lb spaghetti or linguine
  • 4 tablespoons high-quality extra virgin olive oil
  • 4 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • 1/2 teaspoon red pepper flakes (adjust for heat)
  • 1/4 cup fresh Italian parsley, finely chopped
  • 1/2 cup reserved pasta water
  • Freshly grated Pecorino Romano or Parmesan cheese
  • Salt to taste

Instructions

  1. Bring a large pot of heavily salted water to a boil. Cook the pasta until it is just short of al dente (about 1-2 minutes less than package instructions).
  2. While the pasta cooks, combine the olive oil and sliced garlic in a cold, large skillet. Turn the heat to medium-low. Let the garlic gently toast until it becomes fragrant and turns a light golden blonde color (do not burn it!).
  3. Add the red pepper flakes to the skillet and cook for another 30 seconds. Turn off the heat if the pasta isn't ready yet.
  4. Just before draining the pasta, carefully scoop out 1/2 cup of the starchy pasta water and set it aside.
  5. Drain the pasta and add it directly into the skillet with the garlic oil. Pour in about half of the reserved pasta water.
  6. Turn the heat to medium and toss or stir vigorously for 1-2 minutes until the oil and water emulsify into a light, glossy sauce coating the noodles. Add more pasta water if it looks too dry.
  7. Remove from heat, stir in the chopped fresh parsley, top with plenty of grated cheese, and serve immediately.

Why this recipe works

Starting the garlic in cool oil rather than hot draws out its sweetness slowly and gives you control over the colour. The starchy pasta water, added at the end, emulsifies with the oil to make a sauce that coats the spaghetti instead of leaving a puddle in the bowl.

Tips for success

  • Slice the garlic evenly so the pieces brown at the same rate, and keep the heat low.
  • Cook the pasta a minute short of al dente and finish it in the garlic oil with a splash of pasta water.
  • Add the parsley and chili flakes off the heat so they keep their colour and aroma.

Substitutions that work

  • Linguine, bucatini or spaghettoni all work; a bronze-cut pasta holds the sauce better.
  • Pecorino Romano is sharper than parmesan and is the more traditional finish, though either works.
  • Add a handful of toasted breadcrumbs for texture, or a few anchovy fillets melted into the oil for depth.

Storage and make-ahead

Best eaten immediately. Leftovers keep for two days and can be revived in a hot pan with a splash of water, though the garlic will have softened. Do not freeze; the pasta goes mushy.

Serving suggestions

Serve it as a late supper with a glass of something cold, or alongside roast chicken or grilled fish. A squeeze of lemon at the table brightens the whole bowl.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my garlic turn bitter?

The oil was too hot or the garlic cooked too long. Start the garlic in cool oil over low heat and pull the pan as soon as the slices are pale gold.

Can I use pre-minced garlic?

It works but burns more easily and has a sharper flavour. Thinly sliced fresh cloves give the best result.

How much pasta water should I add?

Start with a quarter cup and add more as needed. The sauce should look slightly loose before you add the cheese, because the cheese will thicken it.
