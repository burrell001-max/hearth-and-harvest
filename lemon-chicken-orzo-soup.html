Lemon Chicken Orzo Soup

Prep Time: 15 mins Cook Time: 35 mins Servings: 6 Category: Dinner

This is chicken soup with the volume turned up. The broth is built from the usual vegetables and stock, but the finish is what makes it memorable: eggs whisked with lemon juice and warmed slowly into the pot, which thickens the broth to a light, silky body without any cream at all.

The technique is the same one used in Greek avgolemono, and it takes about two minutes. Get it wrong and you have scrambled egg in soup; get it right and the pot goes from decent to the thing you make when someone is ill.

Ingredients

  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 large onion, diced
  • 2 carrots, diced
  • 2 celery stalks, diced
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1/2 tsp dried thyme
  • 8 cups chicken stock
  • 1 1/4 lb boneless chicken thighs
  • 1 cup orzo
  • 2 large eggs
  • 3 tbsp fresh lemon juice
  • 1 tsp lemon zest
  • 1/4 cup chopped dill
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper

Instructions

  1. Heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onion, carrot and celery and cook for 8 minutes until softened but not browned, then add the garlic and thyme and stir for 1 minute.
  2. Pour in the stock and add the chicken thighs and salt. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook for 18 to 20 minutes, until the chicken reaches 165°F (74°C) and pulls apart easily with two forks.
  3. Lift the chicken out onto a plate and shred it. Add the orzo to the simmering broth and cook for 8 to 9 minutes, until just tender and no longer gritty in the middle.
  4. Whisk the eggs with the lemon juice and zest in a small bowl. With the soup off the heat, ladle a spoonful of hot broth into the egg mixture while whisking, then another, then stir the warmed mixture back into the pot. Do not boil once the eggs go in or they will scramble.
  5. Return the shredded chicken to the pot with the dill, taste for salt and lemon, and serve straight away.

Why this recipe works

Tempering is the whole trick. Whisking hot broth into the eggs a spoonful at a time raises their temperature gradually, so they never hit a temperature that makes them set. Adding the mixture back to a pot that is off the heat finishes the job safely. Shredding the chicken rather than dicing it gives a better texture in the broth, and cooking the orzo in the soup means the starch that would otherwise go down the sink stays in the pot and thickens it.

Tips for success

  • Take the pot off the heat before the egg mixture goes in, and do not let it boil afterwards.
  • Add the lemon juice at the very end. Long cooking flattens the aroma that makes the soup taste fresh.
  • Cook the orzo separately if you plan on leftovers; in the pot it keeps absorbing broth and swells overnight.
  • Use thighs rather than breast. They stay tender through the simmer and shred more easily.
  • Zest the lemon before juicing it, and add the zest with the juice for a sharper, more floral finish.

Substitutions that work

  • Rice, small pasta shapes or pearl couscous can replace the orzo, with timings adjusted to the packet.
  • Two extra egg yolks make the broth richer, closer to the Greek original.
  • Dill can be swapped for parsley or a mixture of both, and a little fresh oregano works well too.
  • For a dairy-free version nothing needs changing, since there is no dairy in the recipe at all.

Storage and make-ahead

Keep for three days in the fridge, but expect the orzo to absorb broth; loosen with stock when reheating and reheat gently without boiling. The soup freezes well for two months if the orzo was cooked separately, and the egg-thickened broth survives freezing better than most cream soups.

Serving suggestions

Serve in deep bowls with plenty of black pepper and a little extra lemon on the side for anyone who wants it sharper. Crusty bread is the obvious accompaniment, and a handful of spinach stirred in at the end turns it into a fuller meal.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my eggs scramble in the soup?

The broth was too hot when the eggs went in, or the pot was put back on the heat afterwards. Temper the eggs slowly with hot broth first, and add them only once the pot is off the stove.

Can I use rice instead of orzo?

Yes, but cook it separately. Rice absorbs far more liquid than orzo and will turn the soup into a porridge if it simmers in the pot.

How do I stop the orzo soaking up all the broth?

Cook the orzo in a separate pan of salted water, drain it and add it to each bowl as you serve. Any soup you are not eating immediately stays brothy.
