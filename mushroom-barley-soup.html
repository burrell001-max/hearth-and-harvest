Mushroom and Barley Soup

Prep Time: 15 mins Cook Time: 50 mins Servings: 6 Category: Dinner

Illustrative image.

Mushroom soup usually tastes of cream and not much else. This one is built on the mushrooms themselves, browned hard in batches until their edges are dark and their water has cooked off, which is where the savoury, almost meaty flavour comes from.

Barley gives it body without flour or a blender. The grains release starch as they simmer, thickening the broth around them until it coats a spoon, and they keep a pleasant chew that stops the soup feeling like a purée.

Ingredients

  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 lb cremini mushrooms, sliced
  • 4 oz shiitake mushrooms, stems removed and caps sliced
  • 1 large onion, diced
  • 2 carrots, diced
  • 2 celery stalks, diced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 3/4 cup pearl barley, rinsed
  • 6 cups vegetable or chicken stock
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 2 tbsp chopped parsley

Instructions

  1. Heat the oil in a heavy pot over medium-high heat. Add the mushrooms in a single layer and leave them for 4 minutes without stirring, then toss and cook for another 4 minutes until deeply browned and shrunken. Doing this in two batches keeps them from steaming.
  2. Add the onion, carrot and celery and cook for 7 minutes until softened. Stir in the garlic, tomato paste and thyme and cook for 1 minute, until the paste darkens.
  3. Add the barley, stock, soy sauce, salt and pepper. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat and simmer partially covered for 35 to 40 minutes, until the barley is tender and the broth has thickened around it.
  4. Taste and adjust the salt; barley absorbs a lot of seasoning as it cooks. If the soup has become too thick, loosen it with a splash of stock or water.
  5. Stir in the parsley and serve with bread, since the soup thickens further as it cools.

Why this recipe works

Mushrooms are mostly water, and they will not brown until that water has evaporated. Giving them room in the pan and leaving them undisturbed is the only way to get there; a crowded pan produces steam and pale, rubbery slices. The tomato paste adds a sweetness and depth that vegetables alone do not provide, and the soy sauce contributes salt plus glutamates that make the mushroom flavour read as stronger than it is.

Tips for success

  • Do not wash the mushrooms. Wipe them with a damp cloth, since soaked mushrooms release water for far longer in the pan.
  • Brown them in two batches, even if it feels slower. One crowded batch will not colour at all.
  • Rinse the barley until the water runs clear, or the soup turns cloudy and gluey.
  • Stir in a splash of stock at the end if the soup has thickened too much; barley keeps drinking liquid as it sits.
  • Season in stages: salt the mushrooms lightly at the start, then adjust again after the barley is tender.

Substitutions that work

  • Any mix of mushrooms works. Button mushrooms alone are fine, though adding a handful of dried porcini, soaked and chopped, deepens the flavour considerably.
  • Pearl barley can be replaced with farro or short-grain rice, both of which cook faster and thicken more.
  • Soy sauce can be swapped for tamari for a gluten-free soup, though the stock and barley also need checking.
  • A tablespoon of dry sherry added with the stock gives a subtle sweetness that suits the mushrooms.

Storage and make-ahead

The soup keeps for four days in the fridge and thickens dramatically as it cools. Reheat with extra stock or water, stirring until it loosens. It freezes for three months, though the barley softens on thawing; add a spoonful of freshly cooked barley when reheating if you want the texture back.

Serving suggestions

Serve with dark bread and butter, or with a spoonful of sour cream swirled in at the table. It makes a good first course before a roast, and a bowl on its own with cheese and bread is a complete winter supper.

Frequently asked questions

Which mushrooms give the best flavour here?

Cremini for the bulk and a smaller amount of shiitake or oyster for depth. Dried porcini, rehydrated in the stock, add more flavour per gram than anything fresh.

Can I use quick-cooking barley?

Yes, and it will cut the simmering time to about 15 minutes, but the soup will be less thick because the grains release less starch. Add a tablespoon of flour with the tomato paste to compensate.

Why does my mushroom soup taste watery and bland?

The mushrooms were not browned properly and the pot was under-seasoned. Brown them in batches until the edges are genuinely dark, and taste for salt after the barley is cooked.
