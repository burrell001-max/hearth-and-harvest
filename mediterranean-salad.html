Mediterranean Chickpea Salad

Prep Time: 15 mins Cook Time: 0 mins Servings: 4 Category: Healthy

Illustrative image.

Chickpea salad is the lunch that survives being made in advance. Canned chickpeas, cucumber, cherry tomatoes, olives and red onion sit in a lemon and oregano dressing, and unlike a lettuce salad, it is arguably better on the second day.

It takes fifteen minutes and makes four generous portions. The feta goes in at the end so it stays in distinct pieces rather than dissolving into the dressing.

Ingredients

  • 2 cans (15 oz each) chickpeas, rinsed and drained
  • 1 large English cucumber, diced
  • 1 cup cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 1/2 cup Kalamata olives, pitted and halved
  • 1/2 cup crumbled feta cheese
  • 1/4 cup red onion, finely diced
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 2 tbsp fresh lemon juice
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • Salt and pepper to taste

Instructions

  1. In a large mixing bowl, combine the drained chickpeas, diced cucumber, cherry tomatoes, Kalamata olives, and red onion.
  2. In a small measuring cup, whisk together the olive oil, lemon juice, dried oregano, salt, and pepper to create the dressing.
  3. Pour the dressing over the salad and toss everything thoroughly to combine.
  4. Gently fold in the crumbled feta cheese right before serving to keep it from dissolving entirely. Serve cold.

Why this recipe works

Chickpeas are sturdy enough to absorb dressing without going limp, and they bring protein and substance that a leaf salad lacks. Salting the cucumber and tomatoes briefly and draining them keeps the salad from turning watery by lunchtime.

Tips for success

  • Rinse the chickpeas well and pat them dry so the dressing clings instead of sliding off.
  • Dice the cucumber and onion small enough that every forkful gets a bit of everything.
  • Add the feta and fresh herbs just before serving.

Substitutions that work

  • White beans or cannellini beans can replace half the chickpeas for a softer texture.
  • Leave out the feta and add extra olives for a vegan version.
  • Add cooked quinoa or farro to turn it into a more substantial grain salad.

Storage and make-ahead

Keeps covered in the refrigerator for four days, and the flavour improves after a few hours. Stir before serving and add a squeeze of lemon if it has been sitting. Do not freeze, as the cucumber loses its texture.

Serving suggestions

Serve as a packed lunch, alongside grilled chicken or salmon, or as part of a mezze spread with hummus and warm pita.

Dressing and resting the salad

The dressing is deliberately simple: good olive oil, lemon juice, oregano, salt and pepper. Use about three parts oil to one part lemon, and whisk it in a small jar rather than a bowl so you can shake it again later. Pour it over the salad, toss well and let it sit for fifteen minutes before serving, which gives the chickpeas time to absorb the lemon and oregano. Taste again after resting; the salad often needs another pinch of salt or a squeeze of lemon once the dressing has been soaked up. Add the feta last so it stays in visible pieces.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make this salad ahead?

Yes, and it is one of the better salads for it. Keep the feta and herbs separate and stir them in just before serving.

How do I stop the salad getting watery?

Drain and rinse the chickpeas, salt and drain the cucumber for ten minutes, and keep the dressing slightly sharp so it does not taste diluted.

Is this salad vegan?

As written it contains feta, so leave it out or use a dairy-free alternative. Everything else in the recipe is plant-based.
