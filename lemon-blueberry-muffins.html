Lemon Blueberry Bakery Muffins

Prep Time: 15 mins Cook Time: 20 mins Servings: 12 muffins Category: Breakfast

Lemon and blueberry is a pairing that works because the acid keeps the fruit from tasting flat. These muffins use sour cream for tenderness, the zest of a whole lemon for perfume, and a full cup and a half of berries so every muffin has more than one.

They bake at 400 F for a tall, domed top and take about twenty minutes. Twelve muffins, and they freeze better than most baked goods.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 2 tsp baking powder
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 cup sour cream or Greek yogurt
  • 1/2 cup vegetable oil
  • Zest and juice of 1 large lemon
  • 1 1/2 cups fresh blueberries

Instructions

  1. Preheat oven to 400°F (200°C) and line a 12-cup muffin tin with paper liners.
  2. In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt.
  3. In another bowl, whisk the eggs, sour cream, oil, lemon juice, and lemon zest together until combined.
  4. Pour the wet ingredients into the dry ingredients, gently folding with a spatula until just mixed (do not overmix!). Fold the fresh blueberries gently into the thick batter.
  5. Divide batter evenly among muffin cups. Bake for 18-20 minutes until muffin tops are golden and spring back when lightly touched.

Why this recipe works

Sour cream adds fat and acidity, which makes the crumb tender and gives the baking powder a chance to lift properly. Tossing the blueberries in a spoonful of flour stops them sinking, and a hot oven sets the edges before the centre has a chance to spread.

Tips for success

  • Rub the lemon zest into the sugar with your fingertips before mixing, which releases more oil.
  • Fold the berries in last, with just two or three strokes, to avoid streaking the batter purple.
  • Fill the liners nearly full for a proper bakery dome.

Substitutions that work

  • Greek yogurt can replace the sour cream one for one.
  • Frozen blueberries work directly from frozen, adding two or three minutes to the bake.
  • Orange zest and dried cranberries make a good winter variation.

Storage and make-ahead

Keep in an airtight container at room temperature for two days, then refrigerate. Reheat for ten seconds to soften. Freeze for three months and thaw at room temperature, or warm from frozen in a low oven.

Serving suggestions

Serve warm with butter and coffee, or pack them for breakfast on the go. They pair well with a fruit salad or a bowl of yogurt.

Zest, sugar and the oven

Most of the lemon flavour in these muffins comes from the zest rather than the juice. Rub the zest into the sugar with your fingertips until the sugar looks damp and smells strongly of citrus; that releases the oils that survive baking. Measure the flour by spooning it into the cup and levelling it, because a packed cup makes the muffins dry and dense. Ovens vary more than people expect, so check the first batch at eighteen minutes and note where the tops sit in your oven. A light-coloured metal tin browns the bases more gently than a dark nonstick one.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my muffins turn out dense?

Overmixing is the usual cause. Stir until the flour just disappears, and fold the berries in gently at the end.

Can I use dried blueberries?

Yes, but rehydrate them in hot water for ten minutes and pat dry first, or they will be hard and chewy in the baked muffin.

How do I keep the lemon flavour strong?

Use zest as well as juice. The zest holds the oils that carry the lemon flavour through baking, and it survives the oven better than juice alone.
