Mixed Berry Baked Oatmeal

Prep Time: 10 mins Cook Time: 35 mins Servings: 6 Category: Breakfast

Illustrative image.

Baked oatmeal sits somewhere between porridge and a coffee cake. Rolled oats soak up milk and egg, bake into a soft, sliceable centre, and the berries on top burst and stain the surface with juice. It is sweet but not dessert-sweet, and it reheats better than almost any other breakfast.

The whole thing mixes in one bowl and bakes in a single dish. Assemble it the night before, bake it in the morning, and you have breakfast for six with very little washing up.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups old-fashioned rolled oats
  • 1/3 cup pure maple syrup or honey
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 2 cups milk (any variety)
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tbsp butter, melted
  • 2 tsp vanilla extract
  • 2 cups fresh or frozen mixed berries

Instructions

  1. Preheat oven to 375°F (190°C) and lightly grease an 8x8-inch baking dish.
  2. In a large bowl, mix together the rolled oats, baking powder, cinnamon, and salt.
  3. In a separate bowl, whisk together the milk, maple syrup, egg, melted butter, and vanilla extract.
  4. Scatter half of the mixed berries along the bottom of the prepared baking dish. Cover evenly with the dry oats mixture, then pour the wet ingredients over the oats.
  5. Scatter the remaining berries over the top. Bake for 35 minutes until the top is beautifully golden brown and the oatmeal is completely set. Serve warm!

Why this recipe works

Egg and a little baking powder give the oats enough structure to cut into squares instead of collapsing into mush. Maple syrup sweetens without the metallic edge of white sugar, and frozen berries work just as well as fresh because they release juice as they bake.

Tips for success

  • Use old-fashioned rolled oats. Instant oats turn to paste and steel-cut oats will not soften in the oven.
  • Scatter the berries on top rather than stirring them through, so they stay intact and the oatmeal does not turn grey.
  • Let the dish rest for ten minutes after baking so the centre sets before you cut into it.

Substitutions that work

  • Any milk works, including almond, oat and soy. Full-fat dairy gives the richest result.
  • Honey can replace the maple syrup, though it browns faster, so drop the oven temperature slightly.
  • Raspberries, blueberries and chopped strawberries are all good, and a handful of chopped apple or pear is a nice addition in autumn.

Storage and make-ahead

Refrigerate leftovers covered for four days. Reheat squares in the microwave for a minute, or in a 325 F oven for ten minutes, with a splash of milk to keep them moist. Freeze cut squares for up to three months and reheat from frozen.

Serving suggestions

Serve it warm with a spoonful of yogurt and a drizzle of maple syrup, or cold as a grab-and-go breakfast. It travels well in a lunchbox and needs nothing alongside except coffee.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make baked oatmeal ahead of time?

Yes. Assemble the dish, cover it and refrigerate overnight, then bake in the morning. Add five minutes to the baking time since the mixture starts cold.

Can I use frozen berries?

Yes, and there is no need to thaw them. Frozen berries hold their shape better and release less juice into the oats than fresh ones.

Is baked oatmeal healthy?

It is a whole-grain breakfast with fibre from the oats and fruit, but sweetness and portion size still matter. This recipe uses a modest amount of maple syrup across six servings.
