Vegetable Curry

Prep Time: 15 mins Cook Time: 25 mins Servings: 4 Category: Healthy

Illustrative image.

This vegetable curry is a one-pot dinner built on coconut milk, curry powder, garlic and ginger. Potatoes and cauliflower go in first because they need the longest cooking, chickpeas go in next, and spinach is stirred through at the end so it keeps its colour.

It serves four with rice and takes about forty minutes. The spice level is mild as written, so add chili if you want heat, and the whole thing reheats well the next day.

Ingredients

  • 2 tbsp coconut oil
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger, minced
  • 2 tbsp curry powder
  • 1 can (14 oz) coconut milk
  • 1 cup vegetable broth
  • 2 potatoes, cubed
  • 1 cup cauliflower florets
  • 1 cup chickpeas, drained and rinsed
  • 1 cup spinach
  • Salt, to taste
  • Steamed rice, for serving

Instructions

  1. Heat coconut oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the onion and cook for 4-5 minutes until softened.
  2. Stir in garlic, ginger, and curry powder, cooking for 1 minute until fragrant.
  3. Pour in the coconut milk and vegetable broth, stirring to combine.
  4. Add the potatoes and cauliflower. Bring to a simmer, cover, and cook for 15 minutes until the vegetables are tender.
  5. Stir in the chickpeas and cook for another 5 minutes.
  6. Fold in the spinach until wilted, season with salt to taste, and serve hot over steamed rice.

Why this recipe works

Frying the curry powder with the onion, garlic and ginger for a minute before adding liquid blooms the spices and removes the raw, dusty taste that happens when powder is simply stirred into coconut milk. Potatoes thicken the sauce naturally as they cook.

Tips for success

  • Cook the onion until soft and golden before adding the spices; this is where the sweetness comes from.
  • Add the coconut milk and broth, then simmer gently rather than boiling, so the coconut milk does not separate.
  • Stir in the spinach at the end and take the pot off the heat as soon as it wilts.

Substitutions that work

  • Sweet potato, butternut squash or green beans can replace the cauliflower or potatoes.
  • Red curry paste can replace the curry powder; use a tablespoon and adjust the salt.
  • Add cooked chicken, shrimp or paneer at the end for a non-vegetarian version.

Storage and make-ahead

Refrigerate for four days, and the flavour improves overnight. Reheat gently on the stove, adding a splash of water if it has thickened. It freezes well for three months; thaw overnight and reheat slowly.

Serving suggestions

Serve over basmati rice, with naan or roti for scooping, and a spoonful of yogurt to cool the spice. A cucumber salad on the side adds freshness.

Layering the pot

The order the vegetables go into the pot determines whether they finish cooking at the same time. Potatoes take the longest, so they go in with the coconut milk and broth and simmer for ten minutes before the cauliflower follows. Chickpeas are already cooked and only need to heat through, so they go in near the end, and the spinach goes in last, off the heat, where it wilts in the residual warmth. If you are using a firm vegetable such as carrot, cut it small and add it with the potatoes. Taste the sauce before serving and adjust the salt.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make this curry vegan?

As written it is vegan, provided the curry powder and broth are plant-based. Check the labels if that matters to you.

Why is my curry watery?

The vegetables released water or too much broth was added. Simmer uncovered for the last ten minutes to reduce it, or mash a few pieces of potato into the sauce.

Can I use frozen vegetables?

Yes. Add them later in the cooking so they do not turn mushy, and reduce the broth slightly since frozen vegetables release water.
