Creamy Gourmet Mushroom Risotto

Prep Time: 10 mins Cook Time: 30 mins Servings: 4 Category: Dinner

Illustrative image.

Risotto has a reputation for being fussy, but the technique is simple: toast the rice, add warm broth a ladle at a time, and stir enough to release starch without scrubbing the grains. The result is a loose, creamy risotto that spreads slowly when it hits the plate.

Mushrooms do most of the flavouring here. A pound of cremini, shiitake or button mushrooms, browned properly before the rice goes in, gives the dish a savoury depth that no amount of parmesan can fake.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups Arborio rice
  • 4 cups low-sodium vegetable or chicken broth (warmed)
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 lb mixed mushrooms (cremini, shiitake, or button), sliced
  • 1 small shallot, finely minced
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1/2 cup dry white wine
  • 3 tbsp unsalted butter
  • 1/2 cup freshly grated Parmesan cheese
  • Salt, pepper, and fresh thyme to taste

Instructions

  1. In a saucepan, heat the broth over low heat and keep it warm throughout the cooking process.
  2. Heat olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add sliced mushrooms and cook until browned and moisture has evaporated (about 5-6 minutes). Remove half of the mushrooms for garnish later.
  3. Reduce heat to medium. Stir minced shallots and garlic into the pan with the remaining mushrooms, cooking for 2 minutes until soft.
  4. Add the Arborio rice, stirring constantly for 1-2 minutes until the edges of the grains become translucent. Pour in the white wine and cook until entirely absorbed.
  5. Add a ladleful of the warm broth to the rice. Stir frequently until the liquid is almost completely absorbed before adding another ladleful. Repeat this process for 20-25 minutes until the rice is creamy but still slightly al dente.
  6. Remove from heat. Stir in the butter, Parmesan cheese, remaining mushrooms, and fresh thyme. Season with salt and pepper to taste, then serve hot.

Why this recipe works

Arborio rice releases starch as it cooks, which is what creates the creaminess without actual cream. Keeping the broth warm means the rice never stops cooking, and adding it gradually lets you judge the texture rather than guessing at a total cooking time.

Tips for success

  • Brown the mushrooms in a wide pan without crowding them, and salt them only once they have colour.
  • Keep the broth at a low simmer in a separate pot and add it one ladle at a time.
  • Take the risotto off the heat while it still looks slightly loose; it tightens as it rests.

Substitutions that work

  • Carnaroli rice holds up even better than Arborio if you can find it. Do not use long-grain rice, which will not cream up.
  • Dry white wine can be replaced with an extra half cup of broth and a squeeze of lemon.
  • For a vegan version, use olive oil instead of butter and nutritional yeast or a dairy-free parmesan alternative.

Storage and make-ahead

Risotto is best eaten immediately. Leftovers keep for two days in the refrigerator and can be reheated with a splash of broth, though the texture will be firmer. Leftover risotto also makes excellent fried rice balls or arancini.

Serving suggestions

Serve it in warm bowls with extra parmesan, black pepper and a drizzle of good olive oil. A simple green salad or a plate of roasted vegetables is all it needs on the side.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to stir risotto constantly?

No. Stirring every minute or so is enough. Constant stirring makes the rice creamier but is not required, and the rice needs the liquid absorbed between additions more than it needs agitation.

Can I use a different rice?

Arborio and Carnaroli are the right choices. Short-grain pudding rice will work in a pinch, but long-grain rice gives a soupy, separate-grain result.

Why is my risotto crunchy in the middle?

The broth was added too quickly or the heat was too high, so the outside cooked before the centre. Add the liquid gradually and keep the pan at a steady simmer.
